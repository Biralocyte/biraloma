# Suggested articles (not in the zip). Optional; the talk does not depend on them.
Citations are copied as printed in the source reference lists (the source is given in brackets).

1. Goel A. Basilar invagination, Chiari malformation, syringomyelia: a review. Neurol India. 2009;57(3):235-246. [Chong Ch 14 refs]
   Why: Goel's instability-based types of basilar invagination (the "type A/B" vocabulary neurosurgeons use). The talk uses the equivalent Liu & Chen two-group description instead, because Goel's classification is not in the sources.
2. Smoker WR, Khanna G. Imaging the craniocervical junction. Childs Nerv Syst. 2008;24:1123-45. [AIIMS ref 35]
   Why: newer, well-illustrated imaging review. Likely to fill the craniometric gaps (basion–dens and basion–axial intervals, Powers ratio, condyle–C1 interval) with CT-era values and images.
3. Anastasia VD, Alexander B, Julian A, et al. Radiologic spectrum of craniocervical distraction injuries. Radiographics. 2000;20:S237-50. [AIIMS ref 32, author names exactly as printed; they appear garbled in the source]
   Why: imaging of atlanto-occipital distraction, with images and interval measurements. The talk currently has only a mummy CT for AOD.
4. Dlouhy BJ, Policeni BA, Menezes AH. Reduction of atlantoaxial dislocation prevented by pathological position of the transverse ligament in fixed, irreducible os odontoideum: operative illustrations and radiographic correlates in 41 patients. J Neurosurg Spine. 2017;27(1):20-28. [Youmans refs]
   Why: imaging predictors of irreducibility, which directly changes surgical planning.
5. Teegala R, Kumar P, Kale S, et al. Craniovertebral junction tuberculosis: a new comprehensive therapeutic strategy. Neurosurgery 2008;63:946-55. [Chen ref 58]
   Why: TB is common locally, but no source in the zip has a TB image or a management strategy.
