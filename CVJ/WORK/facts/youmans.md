# Facts: Youmans & Winn 8th ed., Ch. 260 (Menezes, Ahmed, Dlouhy). Page numbers = PDF page (PDF p3 = printed 1957; p19-21 = refs).
Note: chapter is pediatric/developmental. Not present in source: Anderson-D'Alonzo, Traynelis, Jefferson/Landells, Levine-Edwards/Effendi, Anderson-Montesano, Tuli/Fielding RA, Goel types, rheumatoid, TB, neoplasm, degenerative CVJ classifications. Only passing mentions listed below.

## Anatomy & ligaments
- "The term craniovertebral junction refers to the occipital bone that surrounds the foramen magnum and the atlas and axis vertebrae." [youmans p.3]
- "The atlantoaxial complex is unique among the intervertebral joints, in that it is horizontally oriented. The lateral facet joints are relatively flat and allow for a pivoting motion at the atlantodental articulation" [youmans p.3]
- "The transverse atlantal ligament is a 3- to 5-mm-thick band that originates from the tubercles and the inner aspect of the C1 lateral masses, is in close apposition to the odontoid, and allows axial rotation." [youmans p.3]
- "By itself, the geometry of the craniovertebral complex is meant to provide mobility at the cost of stability." Complex maintained "in a clamp-like vise by the craniovertebral musculature" [youmans p.3]
- Blood supply: "anterior and posterior ascending vessels from the vertebral arteries, with a contribution from the carotid arteries, which form an apical arcade around the alar ligament. Thus the odontoid process blood supply is vulnerable in type II odontoid fracture." [youmans p.3]
- Pharyngovertebral veins "with frequent lymphovenous anastomoses" may provide route for septic involvement; lymphatic drainage "primarily into the retropharyngeal lymph nodes", retrograde infection may cause effusion/instability, "so-called Grisel syndrome." [youmans p.3]
- Embryology: first two occipital sclerotomes form basiocciput; third forms exoccipital center/jugular tubercles; fourth = proatlas: hypocentrum -> anterior tubercle of clivus; centrum -> "apical cap of the dens as well as the apical ligament"; neural arch ventral-rostral -> "U-shaped anterior margin of the foramen magnum, as well as the occipital condyles and the midline occipital condyle"; cruciate and alar ligaments from lateral portion of proatlas; caudal division -> lateral atlantal masses and superior posterior atlas arch. [youmans p.4]
- "at birth, the odontoid process is separated from the body of the axis vertebra by a cartilaginous band... the neural central synchondrosis. This lies below the level of the superior articular facets of the axis and does not represent the anatomic base of the dens. This synchondrosis is present in most children younger than 3 to 4 years and disappears by age 8 years." [youmans p.4]
- "The tip of the odontoid is not ossified at birth and is not seen on lateral radiographs. It is represented by a separate ossification center, which is usually seen at age 3 years and fuses with the remainder of the dens by age 12 years." [youmans p.4]
- Anomalies of caudal occipital sclerotomes "are collectively called 'manifestations of occipital vertebrae'"; clivus segmentation failure -> ventral cervicomedullary indentation with age. [youmans p.5]
- Posterior fossa growth continues "from birth into late adolescence"; ventral bony abnormality may recur after dorsal fusion in first two decades. [youmans p.5]

## Imaging technique
- "The diagnostic procedure of choice is magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), but cervical spine radiographs, with dynamic views, help elucidate craniocervical biomechanics." [youmans p.7]
- "Flexion and extension dynamic views are obtained in parasagittal views in both T1- and T2-weighted modes. The effects of cervical traction are also documented with MRI" [youmans p.7]
- "Magnetic resonance angiography and computed tomographic angiography studies are useful to identify the associated vasculature and recognize any vascular deformities that may occur with motion" [youmans p.7]
- "Three-dimensional CT is useful for defining craniovertebral osseous pathology, especially in identifying epiphyseal growth plates in pediatric patients, as well as for assessing bony fusion and segmentation bony defects." [youmans p.7]
- "Three-dimensional CT combined with MRI is critical" for proatlas segmentation failures; "Hindbrain herniation was present when the posterior fossa volume or vertical height was reduced." [youmans p.11]
- Growth plates: "essential to identify the potential for osseous development by radiographic recognition of epiphyseal growth plates on thin-section CT studies." [youmans p.8]
- Successful reduction "is verified by intraoperative CT with three-dimensional display" [youmans p.8]
- MPS: "CT scans should be reserved for children who have abnormalities on MRI, which also should be done with flexion-extension views." [youmans p.18]
- Os odontoideum: "accurately define the biomechanics... through dynamic studies in the flexed, extended, and neutral positions." [youmans p.14]
- "The unexplained neurological presentations with craniocervical abnormalities warrant dynamic motion studies and vascular imaging." (Key concepts, text with dropped letters) [youmans p.3]

## Craniometry (all lateral/sagittal reference lines, "described before the advent of computed tomography (CT) and MRI"; source does not specify modality per line, Fig. 260.3 is a lateral drawing) [youmans p.7-8]
- Chamberlain palato-occipital line: "joins the hard palate to the posterior edge of the foramen magnum. In normal individuals, the tip of the dens should lie below this line or at most 3 to 5 mm above it. Basilar invagination occurs when the dens projects above this line." [youmans p.7] (Chamberlain 1939, ref 2)
- Wackenheim clivus canal line: "drawn along the clivus and extrapolated into the cervical spinal canal, is of greatest significance. The odontoid process must be ventral or tangential to this line, but transects it in basilar invagination, atlantoaxial dislocation, and anterior occipitoatlantal dislocation." [youmans p.7]
- McRae foramen magnum line: "joins the anterior and posterior edges of the foramen magnum, and the tip of the dens must be below this line in normal individuals." [youmans p.7]
- "Sagittal constriction at the cervicomedullary junction to less than 20 mm in a child older than 8 years is typically associated with a neurological deficit." [youmans p.7]
- Fig. 260.3 lists: "1 Wackenheim's clivus canal line; 2 Chamberlain line; 3 McRae line; 4 McGregor line; 5 Height index of Klaus; 6 Spinous interlaminar line (posterior canal line)" - McGregor, Klaus and spinous interlaminar line are labeled in figure but NOT defined in text; no values given. [youmans p.8]
- Atlantodental interval: "In a child, up to 5 mm of anterior-posterior translation between the dens and the anterior atlas ring is considered normal." Transverse component disrupted: "the intact alar ligaments limit displacement to between 5 and 6 mm." Both incompetent: "anterior-posterior translation exceeds 6 mm. Beyond the age of 8 years, the predental space excursion must be limited to 3 mm." [youmans p.6] (modality not stated; dynamic lateral)
- Down syndrome: "An atlantodental interval greater than 4.5 mm mandates further neurosurgical evaluation." (screening cervical radiographs before sports) [youmans p.10]; Fig 260.6A: pathologic ADI "10 mm" in 5-year-old [youmans p.10]
- Down syndrome surgical consensus: "sagittal plane excursion of more than 8 mm." [youmans p.11]
- Rotation: "Rotation exceeding 50 degrees is associated with a risk of an atlantoaxial facet interlock. If the transverse ligament is injured, the anterior arch can subluxate, leading to a unilateral dislocation facet interlock at less than 40 degrees." "Rotation of more than 35 degrees produces an angulation of the contralateral vertebral artery" [youmans p.6]
- "The atlantoaxial motion segment... allowing a bilateral range of motion of 90 degrees. More than half of all cervical axial rotation occurs at the C1–C2 motion segment" [youmans p.6]
- Platybasia: "an abnormal obtuse basal angle formed by the clivus and the anterior skull base. There are no symptoms or signs attributable to platybasia alone. It is not a measure of basilar invagination" [youmans p.13]
- Posterior fossa: "reduction in posterior fossa volume or vertical height" = hindbrain herniation (no cut-off). [youmans p.11]
- Achondroplasia foramen magnum: "the sagittal diameter of the foramen magnum is preserved and the transverse diameter is marked reduced"; inward bending of exoccipital bone creates "a dural shelf" [youmans p.17]

## Congenital/developmental
- Box 260.1 working classification (Menezes): Malformations of occipital bone (manifestations of occipital vertebra: clivus segmentations, remnants around foramen magnum, atlas variants, dens segmentation anomalies; basilar invagination; condylar hypoplasia; assimilation of atlas); malformations of atlas (assimilation, atlantoaxial fusion, aplasia of atlas arches); malformations of axis (irregular atlantoaxial segmentation; dens dysplasias: ossiculum terminale persistens, os odontoideum, hypoplasia-aplasia; segmentation failure C2/C3); developmental and acquired: secondary basilar invagination (Paget, osteomalacia, rheumatoid cranial settling, renal rickets), foraminal stenosis (achondroplasia); atlantoaxial instability (Morquio, Down, infections e.g. Grisel, inflammatory e.g. RA, traumatic occipitoatlantal/atlantoaxial dislocation, os odontoideum, tumors, misc.). "A working classification has been provided for these diverse pathologies, but is associated with significant overlap" [youmans p.6]
- "84 syndromes have been identified that affect the craniovertebral junction" (over 7000 patients) [youmans p.6]
- "Atlas assimilation has a general incidence of 0.25% and is frequently associated with segmentation failures of the upper cervical spine." [youmans p.6]
- Atlas assimilation: "results from segmentation failure between the fourth occipital sclerotome and the first spinal sclerotome. It may be unilateral, focal, segmental, or bilateral... often leads to secondary basilar invagination." 920 of 7000 in author's database. [youmans p.11]
- Atlas assimilation natural history: pannus around odontoid -> reducible AAI; upward migration of axis -> "reducible basilar invagination up to 14 to 15 years of age"; later irreducible with horizontal clivus. "unlike adults presenting with atlantoaxial dislocation, pediatric patients are likely to have a reducible atlantoaxial dislocation or a reducible basilar invagination" Hindbrain herniation "in 38%". [youmans p.11]
- "An operative procedure that relies on posterior decompression for hindbrain herniation without addressing the potential for instability can have unfortunate results." [youmans p.11]
- Proatlas segmentation failures: "Hindbrain herniation is present in 32% to 38%"; series of 72: "Spastic quadriparesis was seen in 80% and lower cranial nerve palsies (cranial nerves VIII through XII) in 33%... Vertebrobasilar system dysfunction... 40% and trauma presentation in 60%". "Treatment involves precise definition of the abnormality, relief of neurovascular compression, and prevention of instability by instrumented fusion." [youmans p.11]
- Klippel-Feil triad "short neck, webbed neck, and lower hairline with limitation of neck motion"; "30% of patients with Klippel-Feil syndrome have genitourinary abnormalities." [youmans p.11]
- Basilar invagination: "A primary developmental defect implying prolapse of the vertebral column into the skull base"; Chiari I and syringohydromyelia "in 25% to 30% of patients". Two types: ventral ("shortening of the basiocciput so that the clivus is short and horizontally oriented") and paramesial ("condylar hypoplasia may be present so that the clivus becomes dorsally displaced into the posterior fossa and may be of normal length"). "the axis body becomes elongated and the true odontoid process is small." [youmans p.13]
- "Basilar impression is in fact an acquired form of basilar invagination secondary to softening of the skull" (osteogenesis imperfecta, rickets, hyperparathyroidism, Paget, Hurler, Hajdu-Cheney). "The term basilar invagination was used by Chamberlain in 1939 as a synonym for platybasia." [youmans p.13]
- Aplasia-hypoplasia of dens: "Theoretically, the cruciate ligament is incompetent, leading to atlantoaxial instability... quite common in patients with atlas assimilation with failure of C2 and C3 segmentation." [youmans p.14]
- Os odontoideum: "an independent bone cranial to the axis in the place of the dens. It is not an isolated dens; rather, it exists apart from a small hypoplastic dens. Radiographically, an os odontoideum has rounded, smooth cortical borders separated by a variable gap from a small odontoid process." "The gap... usually extends above the level of the superior facets of the axis, leading to incompetence of the cruciate ligament" [youmans p.14]
- Fielding and Griffin: "the orthotopic variety, in which the ossicle lies in the position of the normal dens and moves in unison with the atlas and axis vertebrae; and the dystopic variety, in which the ossicle lies near the inferior end of the clivus, fuses with the occipital bone, and moves in unison with the clivus." "Evidence favors an unrecognized fracture at the odontoid base as the most common cause." Earlier text: "Os odontoideum most likely originates as odontoid trauma that may predate birth but certainly occurs before age 4 years" [youmans p.14, p.5]
- Os odontoideum dystopic: dorsal compression "from anterior displacement of the posterior atlas during flexion"; "the axis may be displaced dorsally in extension, leading to ventral compromise by the axis body." [youmans p.14]
- Irreducible os odontoideum in 41 of >200 operated; "61% were adults; 39% pediatric... neck pain and headaches in the majority (93%)." Irreducibility from "ventral impaction and translocation by the transverse process of the cruciate ligament or because of an exuberant granulation proliferation" [youmans p.14]
- "all patients with recognizable instability at the craniovertebral junction and associated os odontoideum should undergo stabilization. If a fixed irreducible abnormality exists, ventral decompression should be done first." [youmans p.14]
- Basilar impression imaging: "infolding of the squamous portion of the occipital bone; ... posterior fossa floor becomes elevated... basiocciput becomes foreshortened and elevated, with the clivus becoming thinned, truncated, and horizontally oriented, creating an acute craniovertebral angle" [youmans p.15]
- Congenital basilar impression "characteristically relentless in its progression... any therapeutic intervention must be considered palliative"; after fusion "basilar invagination progressed in 80%" despite 100% fusion; fusion mass migrates rostrally and "acts as a fulcrum"; Minerva orthosis recommended. [youmans p.15-17]

## Trauma & classifications
- Os odontoideum/odontoid: see Fielding (above). "odontoid fractures in this age group are usually seen as avulsion injuries with separation of the neural central synchondrosis"; "Spinal trauma in children younger than 8 years is mainly centered at the craniovertebral border due to the high fulcrum of neck motion. This results in ligamentous injuries more often than fractures." [youmans p.5]
- "Transverse ligamentous injury renders C1 grossly unstable and mandates a C1–C2 fusion." "Following injury, the transverse ligament is incapable of repair." [youmans p.6]
- "Failure of one alar ligament results in moderate rotary atlantoaxial instability. Bilateral transection of the alar ligaments causes craniovertebral instability." [youmans p.6]
- "The odontoid process blood supply is vulnerable in type II odontoid fracture." (no classification given) [youmans p.3]

## Infection incl TB (Grisel)
- "Grisel syndrome is a rare pediatric disease named after P. Grisel, who described two cases in 1930. It is defined as an inflammatory, spontaneous subluxation that affects the atlantoaxial joint following parapharyngeal infection." Conditions: "tonsillitis, mastoiditis, retropharyngeal abscess, and otitis media"; "mostly affects children below the age of 12 years"; also after tonsil/adenoid surgery and mastoidectomies. [youmans p.8-9]
- "Magnetic resonance imaging studies help identify the parapharyngeal soft tissue masses if present, as well as the presence of dislocation and osteomyelitis or bone erosion. Needle biopsy of a parapharyngeal mass is essential..." [youmans p.9]
- Management: "Manual reduction... with cervical traction is reserved only for gross dislocations. Otherwise immobilization in a sternal-occipital-mandibular immobilizer is sufficient. However, occipitocervical dislocation requires halo immobilization, and disruption of the craniocervical ligaments, especially the transverse ligament, would require a fusion." [youmans p.9]
- No tuberculosis content in this chapter.

## Syndromic
- Down: "incidence of 1 in 700 live births." "Atlantoaxial instability is present in 14% to 24% of cases, although the incidence of symptomatic atlantoaxial instability is believed to be < 1%"; "atlantoaxial dislocation in up to 20%" (epidemiology). [youmans p.6, p.9-10]
- Down series (Menezes): 110 symptomatic; of 54, 36 operated; 7 (19%) irreducible needing ventral decompression + dorsal OC fusion; 11 (30%) C1-C2 fusion for reducible C1-C2 dislocation; 18 (50%) occipitocervical fusion; 18 os odontoideum; 12 bifid anterior and posterior atlantal arch; 19/54 (35%) complete resolution, 16/54 (29%) significant improvement. "New-onset neurological deficits can occur in up to 1.2%". Consensus surgical indications: "presence of neurological dysfunction, craniovertebral instability associated with a dystopic os odontoideum and/or incomplete atlantal arches, and, finally, sagittal plane excursion of more than 8 mm." [youmans p.10-11]
- Morquio/MPS: "atlantoaxial instability, os odontoideum, and cervicothoracic abnormalities occurs in up to 50% of individuals with Morquio syndrome." [youmans p.6] MPS: "odontoid hypoplasia, peri-odontoid soft tissue masses due to GAG accumulation behind the odontoid process, and cartilage-fibrocartilage reactive hypertrophy, as well as hypertrophy of the dura and ligamentum flavum"; MPS VI: multilevel subaxial stenosis; "hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and enzyme replacement therapy... the presence of spinal abnormalities and deposition of GAG in soft tissue remains unaltered." [youmans p.18]
- Achondroplasia: "most common skeletal dysplasia, 1 in 15,000 to 40,000 live births"; FGFR3; paramesial invagination; foramen magnum narrowing; "Risks of sudden death in children with achondroplasia ... American Academy of Pediatrics suggests screening all infants with achondroplasia using either CT or MRI of the brain and cervical spine." "Posterior decompression is necessary with a duraplasty." Treat hydrocephalus first (third ventriculostomy or VP shunt). [youmans p.17]
- Spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia: support infant with orthosis "until definitive surgical treatment can be performed after the age of 4 years." [youmans p.17]
- Hajdu-Cheney (Fig 260.15) basilar impression with C1 migration. Ehlers-Danlos (Fig 260.2) AAD. Goldenhar: rotary AAD in 26 children. [youmans p.11]; Hajdu-Cheney Fig 260.15 p.16; Ehlers-Danlos Fig 260.2 p.5
- Klippel-Feil: hearing loss 25% of children with deficits ("most common cranial nerve dysfunction is hearing loss, occurring in 25%"). [youmans p.7]

## Degenerative/other
- Achondroplasia subaxial stenosis: "bulging of the intervertebral disks, facet hypertrophy, shortened vertebral bodies, and thickening of the pedicles as a result of premature vertebral arch fusion." [youmans p.17] No degenerative CVJ content otherwise.
- Syringohydromyelia "resolves in up to 76% of patients after ventral decompression" (CSF flow); failure: arachnoid veil, scarring, impacted tonsil. [youmans p.14]

## Instability & management / surgical decision points
- Factors: "Reducibility of the bony lesion; The mechanics of compression and direction of encroachment; The cause of the pathologic process, as well as the presence of hindbrain herniation, syrinx, and vascular abnormalities; The presence of abnormal ossification centers and epiphyseal growth plates" [youmans p.7]
- "The primary aim of treatment is to relieve compression at the cervicomedullary junction. Stabilization is paramount in reducible lesions to maintain neural decompression. Irreducible lesions require decompression and can be subdivided into ventral and dorsal compression states." Ventral: transpalatopharyngeal, Le Fort drop-down maxillotomy, lateral extrapharyngeal; dorsal: posterolateral decompression; "If instability is present after decompression, posterior fixation is mandated." [youmans p.8]
- Fig 260.4 algorithm: MRI/CT +/- flexion/extension MRI -> compression/instability vs none (follow-up); reducible (acute: orthosis; chronic: unstable -> posterior fusion) vs irreducible (neuromuscular blockade + traction, intraop CT); if still irreducible: ventrolateral encroachment: level of nasopharynx (above hard palate) endonasal or transoral; oropharynx (below hard palate) transoral or transcervical; then posterior fusion if unstable; dorsal encroachment: dorsal decompression, fusion if unstable. [youmans p.9]
- Endoscopic: endonasal "enables access to the rostral craniocervical junction while avoiding prolonged postoperative intubation and the risk of oropharyngeal dysfunction"; "surgical access corridor is restricted within the lateral and inferior limits, and pharyngeal closure is not possible"; transcervical "cannot be used in patients with spinal deformity, obesity, or ventral chest wall constraints." [youmans p.8]
- Chiari + BI: "ventral decompression... should be performed before any posterior surgical procedure. Posterior surgical procedures attempted prior to relieving the ventral compression are associated with unfavorable results in up to 30%" (pontomedullary distortion in prone position; flexed fusion hinders later ventral decompression). Syrinx with hindbrain herniation and BI "should not sway the treating neurosurgeon to perform a posterior operative procedure." [youmans p.13]
- Traction: "In children younger than 2 years, 8- to 10-point skull fixation is used with 1 to 2 pounds of pin pressure... 2 and 5 years, maximal pin pressure 4 pounds... older than 5 years, traction initiated at 3 to 4 pounds and does not exceed 7 pounds. In adults, traction starts at 7 to 8 pounds and should not exceed 15 pounds." [youmans p.8]
- Intraoperative reduction of "irreducible" BI under neuromuscular blockade, then posterior decompression and fusion in reduced position. [youmans p.8]
- Fusion: "Atlantoaxial arthrodesis requires a minimum of 3 months of immobilization"; occipitocervical orthosis 5-6 months; "Rates of fusion failure reach 50% when immobilization is inadequate. Wire fixation... should be avoided. Instrumented fusion is undertaken above 6 years of age"; rib graft in younger. [youmans p.8]
- Young children with dysplasias: "In the absence of growth plates, skeletal development can be permitted while externally supporting the occipitocervical region with custom-built cervical orthoses"; "Following bony development of the craniovertebral junction at 3 to 4 years of age, surgical intervention can be undertaken if needed." [youmans p.8]
- Orthoses: "The halo brace is most effective at constraining cervical motion at all levels... best control... at the craniovertebral junction"; soft collar "little immobilization". [youmans p.6]
- Posterior fossa growth "from birth into late adolescence provides a rationale for the need to observe children who have undergone dorsal occipitocervical stabilization or ventral craniovertebral decompression"; downward brain growth/clivus elongation "may re-create a ventral bony abnormality later in life." [youmans p.5]

## Urgent findings
- "Clinical presentation may be insidious or may present as false localizing signs or, infrequently, as a rapid neurological progression followed by sudden death." [youmans p.7]
- "Os odontoideum: It is not uncommon for children with asymptomatic os odontoideum to have severe neurological deficits after minor trauma." [youmans p.14]
- Hearing loss, dysphagia, sleep apnea, downbeat nystagmus ("present in strictly compressive lesions of the craniovertebral border with or without an associated Chiari malformation"); "basilar migraine affects about 25% of children with basilar invagination"; "Vascular symptoms... appear in 15% to 25%"; neck pain "occurring in 85% of patients". [youmans p.7]
- Vertebral artery risk with head rotation >35 degrees (wrestling, football, anesthetic induction, chiropractic manipulation). [youmans p.6]
- Sudden death risk in achondroplasia with cervicomedullary compression. [youmans p.17]
