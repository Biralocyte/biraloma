## GAP FILLERS (CVJ TB grading, imaging, algorithm, outcomes, images)

### Classification / grading (AIIMS CVJ TB grading system) [teegala p.2 (947), Table 1]
Three components, composite score 3-8:
| Parameter | Score |
|---|---|
| Restriction of active neck movement score (footnote b): No | 1 |
| Yes | 2 |
| Motor score: No | 1 |
| Minimal (MRC motor power >=4) | 2 |
| Severe (MRC motor power <=3) | 3 |
| Radiological score: "Retropharyngeal collection >=7 mm in anteroposterior diameter at C2 body level without evidence of bone destruction or radiological instability" | 1 |
| "Retropharyngeal collection evidence of bone destruction (involvement of 1 Dennis vertebral column), thecal sac compression without cord compression, or cord changes" | 2 |
| "Severe bone destruction (involvement of >1 Dennis vertebral column), with cord compression and/or cord signal changes" | 3 |

- Footnotes: "Based on the composite score, patients were divided into 3 grades: Grade 1, score of 3-4 (n = 27); Grade 2, score of 5-6 (n = 36); Grade 3, score of 7-8 (n = 8)." Footnote b: "Patients who could not flex more than 50% of mentosuprasternal distance in a military erect position." [teegala p.2 (947)]
- "Restriction of active neck movements, motor power, and radiological abnormalities were considered in grading the patients. On the basis of these 3 components, patients could attain a composite score ranging from 3 to 8 and were divided into 3 grades (Table 1)." [teegala p.3 (948)]
- Rationale: "We have often noted a discrepancy between the severity of disease assessed radiologically and overall clinical performance status." [teegala p.1 (946)]; "available quantification systems are inadequate because they are based on either clinical or radiological features only." [p.2 (946-947)]
- Functional disability classification (Table 2), used for follow-up because the radiological component changes after surgery [teegala p.2 (947)]: Class 0 No disability, No. 0; Class 1 "Neck pain with or without neck stiffness and restricted neck movements with impairment of ADL" 30; Class 2 "Moderately disabled patients who require some support from others for their ADL" 32; Class 3 "Severely disabled patients who require help for most of their ADL" 8; Class 4 "Moribund patients with extensive systemic tuberculosis" 1. (ADL, activities of daily living.)

### Imaging findings of CVJ TB
- Imaging protocol: "Plain x-rays of the CVJ in the anteroposterior, lateral (flexion, extension, neutral), and transoral projections and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) comprised the initial radiological evaluation on admission. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography of the CVJ was performed by referring surgeons in 5 patients before their admission" [p.3 (948)].
- Instability (defined on x-ray): "The instability at the CVJ was determined by x-rays. Abnormal movement between the anterior surface of the odontoid and posterior margin of the C1 arch of more than 4 mm in children and 3 mm in adults is considered instability or dissociation of the CVJ." [p.3 (948)]
- Bone involvement: "Radiological evidence of bone involvement was seen in 62 (87%) of 71 patients (Table 5). Involvement of the odontoid process and anterior arch of the atlas was the most common pattern." [p.4 (949)]
- Table 5 extent (No. of patients) [p.4 (949)]: Bone: Dens 61; Anterior arch of the atlas 48; Atlantoaxial dissociation 38; Lateral masses of C1 35; Occipital condyles 30; C2 body 17; Clivus 11. Soft tissue: Prevertebral collection 57; Paraspinal collection 38; Epidural collection 26.
- "Eighty-four percent of the patients had a soft tissue collection. In most cases, the epidural collection extended from the clivus to the C2 or C3 vertebra. A prevertebral collection was present in 80%, a paravertebral collection in 54%, and an epidural space collection in 37%. Thecal sac compression was seen in 22 cases (31%). Five patients had cord signal changes; 4 underwent transoral decompression, and 1 had very minimal compression and was therefore managed without decompression." [p.4 (949)]
- "AAD was present in 38 patients on initial presentation. Ill-defined landmarks at the CVJ precluded its identification in the remaining patients." [p.4 (949)]
- Pathognomonic combination: "The combination of erosion of the atlas or axis, AAD, and increased prevertebral soft tissue shadow is pathognomonic of CVJ TB in an endemic area." [p.7 (952)]
- Radiographic lag: "The plain radiographic changes in tubercular spondylitis may lag behind the pathological alterations by as much as 2 to 6 months (9, 27), because radiographic evidence of bone erosion appears only after 50% of the vertebra has been destroyed (24)." Two patients were initially treated as degenerative spondylosis and later diagnosed as CVJ TB. [p.7 (952)]
- CT/MRI: "Computed tomography and MRI studies of the CVJ in the early phases of CVJ TB readily demonstrate bone fragmentation at the vertebral endplates and an associated obliteration of fat planes around the vertebral body in the early evolution of the abscess (16). The presence of multiloculations and a calcified abscess with a thick, enhancing, irregular rim in the presence of vertebral body fragmentation is pathognomonic of TB (19, 34), differentiating it from other lesions at the CVJ, such as rheumatoid arthritis, brucellosis, sarcoidosis, fungal infection, lymphoma, and chordoma (1, 28, 33-35)." [p.7 (952)]
- "MRI is the ideal diagnostic tool to ascertain the presence and extent of compromise of the spinal canal and cord compression as well as to determine the size of the paraspinal abscess, if present (22)." [p.7 (952)]
- Cord compression thresholds cited from others: "Pattisson (31) and Hoffman et al. (16), evaluating spinal MRI, suggested that spinal cord compression of 60% or more resulted in neurological deficits. al-Mulhim et al. (3) proposed that less than 50% narrowing of the cervical spinal canal produces only mild to moderate deficits and more than 75% narrowing causes severe neurological deficits." [p.6-7 (951-952)]
- Clinicoradiological mismatch: "predicting the neurological deficits solely on the basis of the radiological extent of cervicomedullary compression is likely to overestimate the severity of the disease. The neurological deficits are mainly attributable to cervicomedullary compression and instability at the CVJ." Disability may be from "muscle spasm, pain, restricted neck movements, and generalized malaise caused by the local paravertebral collection. Eleven percent of the patients without features of myelopathy still had Class 2 disability." [p.7 (952)]
- Pathogenesis: "CVJ TB is a secondary infection that either begins in the retropharyngeal space with secondary involvement of bone or, rarely, begins in the bone itself. As the disease progresses, it involves the ligaments, causing erosion of the odontoid or C1 vertebra and resulting in unstable articulation between the occiput and C1 (24). Involvement of the occipital condyles or C1 lateral masses leads to AAD predominantly by destruction of transverse and alar ligaments (25, 30)." [p.6 (951)]
- Images: Fig 2 (Grade 3: odontoid destruction + prevertebral shadow on lateral x-ray; axial CT destruction of right occipital condyle; post-op rod and sublaminar wires), Fig 3 (reducible AAD flexion/extension, MRI resolution), Fig 4 (fixed AAD, dotted parallel lines constant atlantoaxial distance), Fig 5 (Grade 1 AAD resolution), Fig 6 (MRI paravertebral collection lower clivus to C4, resolved) - see inv/teegala_figs.csv.

### Management algorithm and decision points (Fig 1, p.2; Fig 7, p.8) 
- All patients: "all patients, irrespective of their grade, were started on a 4-drug ATT regimen (rifampin, 10-20 mg/kg/d; isoniazid, 5-10 mg/kg/d; ethambutol, 15 mg/kg/d, and pyrazinamide, 20-35 mg/kg/d). All 4 drugs were continued for at least 3 months; 3 drugs (ethambutol, isoniazid, rifampin) were continued for 1 year; and in the final 6 months (months 13-18) only two drugs (isoniazid and rifampin) were continued." [p.3 (948)] Total 18 months [p.5 (950)].
- "Grade 1 and 2 patients were initially managed conservatively, whereas Grade 3 patients immediately underwent single-stage transoral decompression and posterior fixation (Fig. 2). In all patients managed either conservatively or surgically, the neck was stabilized in a Philadelphia hard cervical collar until a stable fibro-osseous posterior union occurred." [p.3 (948)]
- Algorithm (Fig 1): Suspected CVJ TB -> confirm diagnosis -> initial grade. Grade 3 -> primary surgery (TOD + PF) -> follow-up. Grades 1/2 -> conservative management, follow-up with Disability Class: poor response or no response -> TOD + PF and recheck diagnosis; good response -> AAD? No AAD -> follow-up; reducible AAD -> posterior fixation; irreducible AAD -> neurological status unstable -> TOD + PF; stable -> follow-up.
- Goals: "1) to avoid new neurological deficits; 2) to avoid morbidity and compromise in the quality of life associated with prolonged, rigid, cumbersome external immobilization, such as with a sternal occipital mandibular immobilizer brace and halo traction; and 3) to prevent sudden death." [p.1 (946)]
- Surgery: Grade 3 patients: "Occipital fixation (C2-C3 in 7 patients and C3-C4 in 1 patient) was performed with a contoured stainless steel rod and wires." [p.5 (950)] (sic as printed)
- "Grade 3 patients underwent direct surgery, irrespective of AAD status, in the best interest of the patient's speedy recovery and to avoid unnecessary prolonged, cumbersome, rigid external fixation." [p.9 (954)]
- Follow-up: "Patients were followed at 4-week intervals for the initial 3 months and every 2 months thereafter. During each visit, flexion and extension x-rays of the CVJ were obtained, and a clinical evaluation was done. MRI was performed in patients who showed no improvement or had clinicoradiological deterioration. Those patients who showed residual AAD during the follow-up period after resolution of the tubercular infection were evaluated by either MRI or computed tomography of the CVJ to assess the status of AAD." [p.3-4 (948-949)]
- Comparison: Behari et al. "proposed a management protocol based on disability grading"; Teegala thinks most such patients "could be followed instead of undergoing primary surgery"; Lifeso and Edwards "recommended a 2-stage procedure with transoral decompression followed by posterior stabilization, with a 2-week interval during which the patient's neck is stabilized by a halo vest." [p.8 (953)]
- "None of the patients in our study required any kind of cumbersome, rigid external fixator such as halo traction and a sternal occipital mandibular immobilizer brace." [p.8 (953)]

### Outcome numbers
- "Of 71 patients, there were 27 Grade 1, thirty-six Grade 2, and 8 Grade 3 patients." "Five Grade 1 and 2 patients (8%) required delayed surgery for reducible atlantoaxial dissociation. The remaining 58 patients (82%) were effectively managed conservatively. The mean follow-up duration was 18.5 +/- 6.2 months. There was no mortality." [p.1 (946) abstract]
- 71 patients (46 males, 25 females), mean age 26.6 +/- 12.7 years (range 3-60); "Seventy-three percent of the patients were younger than 30 years of age"; follow-up 8-40 months (mean 18.5); 2 lost after 8 months, 3 after 12 months. [p.4 (949)] Abstract says children and young adults 70%.
- Of 38 with AAD: 7 Grade 3 (surgery); of remaining 31, 6 had residual AAD: 5 reducible (posterior fixation), 1 nonreducible with stable neurology (Fig 4); "AAD resolved in 25 patients". [p.4 (949)]
- "At the last follow-up, 63 patients (89%) had attained Class 0 disability. Five patients were Class 1, and 2 patients were Class 2. One Class 4 patient improved to Class 3 after surgery ... needed prolonged ventilatory support postoperatively ... weaned off the ventilator with the aid of a tracheostomy." All respiratory distress improved; all 4 bladder problems regained; 3 discharging sinuses healed in 3-4 months; neck pain relieved in 3 to 4 months; "All patients who underwent surgery showed stable fibro-osseous union at the CVJ". [p.5-6 (950-951)]

### Diagnostic criteria (Table 3) [p.3 (948)]
Essential: "Radiological evidence of paravertebral collection with or without bone destruction and instability" 71. Supportive major: 1 Histopathological evidence of tuberculosis 52; 2 Microbiological evidence 24; 3 PCR positivity for acid-fast bacillus from sputum 6. Minor: 1 Associated radiological evidence of pulmonary TB 20 (only 5 sputum positive); 2 ESR >=50 mm/h 52; 3 Positive Mantoux test 36; 4 Constitutional symptoms (nocturnal increase in temperature, >10% weight loss in past 3 mo, loss of appetite) 46; 5 History of TB 16; 6 Clinicoradiological response to ATT 6. "Diagnosis was established when a patient satisfied the essential criteria along with 1 major supportive criterion or at least 4 minor supportive criteria." Retropharyngeal aspiration biopsy in remaining cases; 6 patients on ATT before referral diagnosed on minor criteria. [p.3 (948)]

## Anatomy & ligaments
- "Involvement of the occipital condyles or C1 lateral masses leads to AAD predominantly by destruction of transverse and alar ligaments (25, 30)." [p.6 (951)]
- Autopsy: Pandya "revealed involvement of bone and ligaments by the disease process as possible causes for dislocation. Primary infection of the atlas or axis was rarely the cause of disease." [p.6 (951)]
- "The craniovertebral junction (CVJ) is a complex anatomic region that is vulnerable to a wide spectrum of developmental and acquired anomalies owing to its unique developmental anatomy and high mobility (13)." [p.1 (946)]

## Imaging technique
- See Gap fillers (x-ray projections, MRI, CT, follow-up flexion/extension). Restriction of neck movement tested as inability to flex >50% of mentosuprasternal distance in military erect position [p.2 (947)]. MRI described as ideal for canal/cord/abscess [p.7 (952)].

## Craniometry
- Only measurements in this paper: (1) "Retropharyngeal collection >=7 mm in anteroposterior diameter at C2 body level" (Table 1, radiological score 1; modality not stated explicitly, in context of radiological score) [p.2 (947)]; (2) instability: "Abnormal movement between the anterior surface of the odontoid and posterior margin of the C1 arch of more than 4 mm in children and 3 mm in adults" determined by x-rays [p.3 (948)]; (3) cord/canal compression cut-offs quoted from MRI literature (60%; <50%; >75%) [p.6-7]. No basilar impression lines, angles or other intervals are defined. Basilar impression is mentioned only as a complication: "secondary basilar impression" [p.1 (946)].

## Congenital/developmental
- None specific. CVJ "vulnerable to a wide spectrum of developmental and acquired anomalies" [p.1 (946)]. Two patients initially treated as degenerative spondylosis [p.7 (952)].

## Trauma & classifications
- None. Classification of note is the TB grading above; Dennis columns used in radiological score 2 and 3 (involvement of 1 vs >1 Dennis vertebral column) [p.2 (947)].

## Rheumatoid/inflammatory
- Differential for TB at CVJ: "rheumatoid arthritis, brucellosis, sarcoidosis, fungal infection, lymphoma, and chordoma (1, 28, 33-35)" [p.7 (952)].

## Infection incl TB
- Epidemiology: "Tubercular spondylitis constitutes approximately 1% of all cases of tuberculosis (TB) (5). CVJ TB is extremely rare, constituting only 0.3 to 1% of spinal TB (9, 11, 23)." "Tubercular involvement of the CVJ may cause atlantoaxial dissociation (AAD), secondary basilar impression, epidural abscess, or granuloma leading to cervicomedullary compression" [p.1 (946)]
- Neurological: "In the series of Hsu and Leong (17), the incidence of compressive myelopathy was 42.5%, compared with a 15 to 30% overall incidence of compressive myelopathy seen in TB of the spine at other locations." "Approximately 30 to 70% of patients with CVJ TB have been reported to present with neurological deficits (4)." [p.7 (952)]
- Symptoms (Table 4, No./%) [p.4 (949)]: Neck pain 71 (100); Restriction of active neck movements 55 (77); Constitutional symptoms 46 (65); Motor weakness 34 (48); Neck tilt 27 (38); TB at other sites 23 (32) [Results text says "Twenty-two patients (32%)"; source internally inconsistent]; Sensory loss 22 (31); Lower cranial nerve palsy 12 (17); Respiratory distress 7 (10); Dizziness and/or vertigo 5 (7); Sphincter involvement 4 (6). Mean symptom duration 6.5 +/- 4.5 months (1-26).
- Diagnostic yield: 65 (92%) satisfied definitive major criteria; 44 Grade 1/2 FNA caseating granulomatous inflammation, AFB smear positive in 20; culture growth in 4; sputum PCR positive 6; 5 lymph node biopsies positive; CSF AFB positive in 2 meningitis patients; 8 early-surgery patients pathologically positive. [p.4-5 (949-950)]
- ATT: 18 months used despite WHO 6-8 month regimens: "We adopted this longer medical treatment duration according to Tuli's (38) recommendations for spinal TB." [p.8 (953)]
- "Patients with CVJ TB usually respond well to ATT." "All patients with CVJ TB responded well to ATT regardless of their initial grade." [p.8, p.9]

## Instability & management/surgical decision points
- See Gap fillers. Key: Grade 3 = primary TOD + posterior fixation; Grades 1/2 = ATT + Philadelphia collar; reducible residual AAD on flexion/extension films after resolution -> posterior fixation (5 of 63) ["by 1 year" UNVERIFIED: text gives no time point]; irreducible AAD with stable neurology -> follow; irreducible + unstable -> TOD + PF; poor/no response -> TOD + PF and recheck diagnosis [p.2].
- "Response to ATT presumably involves an enhanced fibrous reaction, which may obviate the need for additional operative stabilization. Yet we were unable to identify the factors responsible for residual AAD in the few patients who required posterior fixation despite the good response to medical treatment." [p.8 (953)]
- Comment (Mummaneni): "patients in whom nonsurgical therapy is tried should be followed closely to ensure that medical treatment works and that they do not deteriorate neurologically over time." [p.10 (955)]; Traynelis: "as with all treatment algorithms, general assessment of the patient is important to develop an appropriate individualized plan." [p.9 (954)]

## Urgent findings
- "Delay in diagnosis may lead to irreversible neurological deficits and even death (12, 32)." [p.1-2 (946-947)]
- "Initial symptoms may be mild and nonspecific and may lead to a delayed diagnosis, sudden neurological deterioration, and even death due to instability of the C1-C2 complex" [p.7 (952)]
- "to prevent sudden death caused by unrecognized neural compression." [p.2 (947)]
- Cord signal change: 5 patients; respiratory distress 7 (10%); lower cranial nerve palsy 12 (17%) [p.4].
