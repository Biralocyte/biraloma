### Goel Group A vs Group B basilar invagination: definitions (the 2009 classification, "based on parameters that determine an alternative treatment strategy")
- Abstract: "Patients with basilar invagination were categorized into two groups based on the presence (Group A) or absence (Group B) of clinical and radiological evidence of instability of the craniovertebral junction." [goel p.1, pr. 235]
- "Standard radiological parameters described by Chamberlain were used to assess the instability of the craniovertebral junction." [goel p.1, pr. 235]
- Group A: "In Group A basilar invagination there was a 'fixed' atlantoaxial dislocation and the tip of the odontoid process 'invaginated' into the foramen magnum and was above the Chamberlain line, McRae line of foramen magnum and Wackenheim's clival line." [goel p.2, pr. 236]
- "The definition of basilar invagination of prolapse of the cervical spine into the base of the skull, as suggested by von Torklus, was suitable for this group of patients [Figure 1]." [goel p.2, pr. 236]
- Group B: "Group B basilar invagination was where the odontoid process and clivus remained anatomically aligned despite the presence of basilar invagination and other associated anomalies. In this group, the tip of the odontoid process was above Chamberlain's line but below McRae's and Wackenheim's lines [Figure 2]." [goel p.2, pr. 236]
- "Essentially, in Group A basilar invagination, there was an element of instability of the region that was manifested by the tip of the odontoid process distancing itself from the anterior arch of the atlas or the lower end of the clivus." [goel p.2, pr. 236]
- "The radiological findings suggested that the odontoid process in Group A patients resulted in direct compression of the brainstem." [goel p.2, pr. 236]
- Chiari: "In some Group A patients there was Chiari malformation, and this feature differentiates the present classification from the earlier classification." [goel p.2-3, pr. 236-237]  (Group A/B is NOT defined by Chiari presence, unlike earlier Group I/II.)
- Group A/B relative frequency: "Group A basilar invagination forms a larger subgroup of patients that are encountered in Indian subcontinent." [goel p.3, pr. 237]
- Key statement: "Understanding these two types of basilar invagination is probably the most crucial factor in understanding the various involved management issues." [goel p.3, pr. 237]
- Abstract on mechanism: "The pathogenesis and clinical features in patients with Group A basilar invagination appeared to be related to mechanical instability, whereas it appeared to be secondary to embryonic dysgenesis in patients with Group B basilar invagination." [goel p.1, pr. 235]
- Group B mechanism: "The basilar invagination thus resulted from the rostral positioning of the plane of the foramen magnum in relation to the brainstem [Figure 2]." [goel p.4, pr. 238]
- Earlier (1997) Group I/II classification, for contrast: "Based on a single criterion of the absence or presence of Chiari malformation, the anomaly was classified into Group I and II respectively." [goel p.2, pr. 236-237]; Group I: "invagination of the odontoid process into the foramen magnum and it indented into the brainstem. The tip of the odontoid process distanced itself from the anterior arch of the atlas or the inferior aspect of the clivus." "The angle of the clivus and the posterior cranial fossa volume were essentially unaffected in these patiens." Group II: "the assembly of the odontoid process, anterior arch of the atlas and the clivus migrated superiorly in unison resulting in reduction of the posterior cranial fossa volume, which was the primary pathology". "The Chiari malformation or herniation of the cerebellar tonsil was considered to be a result of reduction in the posterior cranial fossa volume." [goel p.3, pr. 237] (190 patients, 1988-97 [goel p.2, pr. 236])

### Atlantoaxial instability concept
- "We had speculated that basilar invagination is secondary to abnormally inclined alignment of the facets of the atlas and axis. The progressive slippage of the atlas over the axis secondary to this malalignment, a process similar to spondylolisthesis in the lumbosacral spine, results in invagination of the odontoid process into the craniocervical cord." [goel p.2, pr. 236]
- "Pain, restriction of neck movements and hyperlordosis of the neck indicate the presence of instability of the craniovertebral junction." [goel p.4, pr. 238]
- "Neck pain, muscle spasms, and restriction of neck movements are frequently noted, suggesting instability of the region." [goel p.2, pr. 236]
- Fixed vs mobile: "Actual mobility of the atlantoaxial joint on flexion and extension of the neck can be demonstrated only rarely. In our recent study, we identified patients in whom there was 'vertical mobile and reducible atlantoaxial dislocation', wherein there was basilar invagination when the neck was flexed, and the alignment was normal when the head was in extended position. Although such mobility is only rarely identified, it does indicate the need for dynamic flexion-extension studies to preoperatively assess the craniovertebral instability." [goel p.4, pr. 238]
- Trauma as marker of instability: "The fact that trauma influenced the acute development of symptoms pointed towards an element of instability of the craniovertebral region in Group A patients." [goel p.6, pr. 240] and "Majority of patients (58%) with Group A basilar invagination had a history of minor to major head injury prior to the onset of the symptoms." [goel p.6]
- Syringomyelia chain: "the complex of atlantoaxial dislocation, basilar invagination and syringomyelia are probably secondary to the primary craniovertebral instability." [goel p.10-11, pr. 244-245]

### Facet orientation
- "In this group, the atlantoaxial joints were 'active' and their orientation was oblique as shown in Figure 3, instead of the normally found horizontal orientation. We have found similarities of such a position of the C1-2 facets with spondylolisthesis seen in the subaxial spine." [goel p.3, pr. 237]
- "It appears to us that the atlantoaxial joint in such patients is in an abnormal position as a result of mechanical instability and progressive worsening of the dislocation is probably secondary to increasing 'slippage' of the facets of atlas over the facets of axis." [goel p.3, pr. 237]
- "In Group B, the atlantoaxial joints were normal and were normally aligned. In some patients the joints were entirely fused." [goel p.3, pr. 237]
- "...listhesis of C1 over C2 is probably the cause of basilar invagination and related bone and soft tissue dysgenesis may not be a congenital defect, but can be a result of mechanical 'incompetence' of the atlantoaxial joints." [goel p.4, pr. 238] (Figure 3 caption: "CT scan showing the listhesis of C1 facet over C2 facet. This listhesis of C1 over C2 is probably the cause of basilar invagination" [goel p.4])
- Syrinx: "It appears that when the angulation of the facets is not as acute or is only marginally affected, the progress of basilar invagination is slow and over several years, providing an opportunity for the syrinx to develop relentlessly." [goel p.10, pr. 244]
- Post-op: "The technique resulted in realignment of facets into the horizontal position and in realignment of the entire craniovertebral junction." [goel p.7, pr. 241]

### Surgical implications: atlantoaxial fixation vs decompression
- Abstract: "Treatment by facetal distraction and direct lateral mass fixation can result in restoration of craniovertebral and cervical alignment in patients with Group A basilar invagination. Such a treatment can circumvent the need for transoral or posterior fossa decompression surgery. Foramen magnum bone decompression appears to be a rational surgical treatment for patients having Group B basilar invagination." [goel p.1, pr. 235]
- "The division of patients with basilar invagination on the basis of presence or absence of instability provides insight into the pathogenesis of the anomaly and a basis for rational surgical treatment." [goel p.1, pr. 235]
- "Our current experience with the technique of over 200 patients, has convinced us that distraction and direct lateral mass fixation of the atlantoaxial joint is the ideal treatment in patients with Group A basilar invagination and transoral surgery can be entirely avoided." [goel p.6-7, pr. 240-241]
- Standard approach: "The standard and most accepted form of treatment of Group A basilar invagination is a transoral decompression. The majority of the authors recommend a posterior occipitocervical fixation following the anterior decompression." [goel p.7, pr. 241]
- "the long-term clinical outcome following the twin operation of transoral decompression followed by posterior stabilization was seen to be inferior to the clinical outcome following our current operation that involves craniovertebral realignment without any bone, dural or neural decompression." [goel p.7]
- "We had earlier attempted to reduce basilar invagination by performing occipitocervical fixation following institution of cervical traction. However, all the four patients treated in this manner subsequently needed transoral decompression as the reduction of the basilar invagination and of atlantoaxial dislocation could not be sustained by the implant." [goel p.7, pr. 241]
- "All patients with Group A basilar invagination underwent joint manipulation surgery [Figures 6, 7]. No patient underwent a transoral decompression as a first-stage operation." [goel p.7]
- Technique: cervical traction "prior to induction of anesthesia and the weights are progressively increased to approximately one-fifth of the total body weight. The patient is placed prone with the head end of the table elevated to about 35 degrees." [goel p.7]; joint capsule excised, cartilage "widely removed using microdrill", distraction with osteotome, "Corticocancellous bone graft harvested from the iliac crest is stuffed into the joint", titanium spacer or hydroxyappatite blocks; "A two-holed stainless steel plate is used measuring 15-20 mm in length. The screws are 2.4 to 2.6 mm in diameter and measure 16-22 mm in length." [goel p.9, pr. 243]; fallbacks: "C1-C2 transarticular screw fixation method described by Magerl or an occipitoaxial fixation" [goel p.9-10]; "four-post hard cervical collar for three months" [goel p.9].
- Results: "In three patients, the reduction of the basilar invagination was not entirely satisfactory and a delayed transoral surgery was performed to decompress the region. No patient needed a re-exploration for failure of fixation of the implant." [goel p.9, pr. 243]. "The extent of distraction of the joint and the subsequent reduction in the basilar invagination was more significant in younger than in older patients." [goel p.9] "the tip of the odontoid process receded in relationship to Wackenheim's clival line, Chamberlain's line and McRae's line suggesting reduction in the basilar invagination." [goel p.9]
- Group B: "patients in Group B benefited by foramen magnum bony decompression. The procedure resulted in amelioration of symptoms and at least an arrest in the progression of the disability. None of the patients in this group had a delayed worsening in the neurological condition following a foramen magnum decompression." [goel p.11, pr. 245]; "Opening of the dura is not only unnecessary but also subjects the patient to an increased risk of cerebrospinal fluid fistula." [goel p.11, pr. 245] "Current papers do not recommend resection of the herniating tonsils" [p.11]
- Final: "The treatment of basilar invagination must be based on the understanding of its pathogenesis. As complications related to an inappropriate treatment can be devastating in nature, exact anatomical and biomechanical evaluation and precise surgical treatment is mandatory in these patients." [goel p.11, pr. 245]

## Congenital/developmental
- "Craniovertebral anomalies are more frequently found in the Indian subcontinent than anywhere else in the world." [goel p.1]
- "The database of the department included 3300 patients with craniovertebral junction pathology from the year 1951 till date." [goel p.1, pr. 235]
- Theories: "mechanical or embryological dysgenesis, genetic abnormalities, and viral infections." [goel p.2, pr. 236]
- von Torklus and Gehle "suboccipital dysplasias". [goel p.2]
- "Short neck, low hairline, web-shaped neck muscles, torticollis, reduction in the range of neck movements ... A number of bone fusion deformities and platybasia have also been recorded." [goel p.2]
- "Klippel-Feil bone anomaly refers to the triad of observations of extensive cervical vertebral fusions, low hairline, restrictions of neck movements and a short neck." [goel p.6, pr. 240]
- Occipitalization: "noted first by Rakitansky [cited by Grawitz 1880]"; "The assimilation of atlas can be partial or incomplete." [goel p.5, pr. 239]
- Platybasia: "The association of platybasia and basilar invagination is well known." "The superior position of the odontoid process was associated with a more horizontal angulation and shortening of the clivus." "Platybasia did not directly result in any neurological symptoms, but it participated with basilar invagination in critically reducing the posterior cranial fossa volume." [goel p.4-5]
- Reversibility: "A number of these abnormalities were seen to be reversible following decompression and stabilization of the region." [goel p.4] "reversal of platybasia in Group A patients following the craniovertebral realignment surgery" [goel p.5]. Study of 170 patients (1999-April 2008): "assimilation of atlas (72%), single level (29%) or multiple-level (3%) cervical fusions" [goel p.10]. "a short neck, exaggerated neck lordosis, torticollis, cervical spondylotic changes and fusions are potentially reversible" [goel p.11]; "neural compression at the craniovertebral junction is primary and the majority of physical and osseous abnormalities are probably secondary in nature." [goel p.11]
- Chiari/syrinx: "Chiari malformation and syringomyelia are common associates of basilar invagination and are the soft tissue components of the dysgenesis." [goel p.1]; "syringomyelia is a tertiary response to primary craniovertebral anomaly in the form of basilar invagination which leads to secondary Chiari 1 malformation as a result of reduction in the posterior cranial fossa volume." [goel p.10, pr. 244]; "Chiari 1 malformation and secondary syringomyelia frequently are not associated with basilar invagination." [goel p.11]
- Syrinx clinical: "Presence of spastic quadriparesis in all our patients suggests that the symptoms were related to the compression of the brainstem from invaginated dens rather than due to syringomyelia." [goel p.10] Group A BI with syrinx "was less severe and symptoms were longstanding" [goel p.11].
- Clinical numbers (Group A): "Majority of patients (58%) ... history of minor to major head injury"; "Kinesthetic sensations were affected in 55% of patients. Spinothalamic dysfunction was less frequent (36%). Neck pain was a major presenting symptom in 77% of patients. Torticollis was present in 41% of patients." [goel p.6, pr. 240]

## Instability & management/surgical decision points
- Decision point: instability (fixed AAD, odontoid distanced from anterior arch/clivus, tip above Chamberlain, McRae, Wackenheim) -> Group A -> distraction of C1-2 facets + lateral mass plate/screw fixation, no decompression, "transoral surgery can be entirely avoided" [goel p.6-7].
- No instability (odontoid-clivus aligned, tip above Chamberlain but below McRae/Wackenheim) -> Group B -> foramen magnum bone decompression; dural opening "not necessary" [goel p.11].
- Group A + syringomyelia: "No bony or dural decompression or neural manipulation of any kind is done in these patients." [goel p.10, pr. 244]; reduction + lateral mass fixation.
- Chiari 1 + syringomyelia without BI: "Our experience suggests that only bony decompression of the foramen magnum is sufficient even in patients with syringomyelia." [goel p.11]; "the treatment even in such patients is only foramen magnum bone decompression." Cited: Logue: muscle plugging "did not seem to change the results"; Di Lorenzo et al: "'conservative' craniocervical decompression should be considered the first option in the treatment of syringomyelia-Chiari 1 complex." [goel p.11, pr. 245]
- Surgery difficulty: "The exposure of the atlantoaxial joint in patients with basilar invagination is significantly more difficult and technically challenging when compared to a normally aligned atlantoaxial joint encountered during the treatment of post-traumatic instability. The joint is significantly rostral in location and the microscope needs to be appropriately angled." [goel p.7]; "The exposure of the facet of the atlas was significantly difficult as in a number of patients there was an assimilation of the atlas resulting in rostrally located C1 facet." [goel p.9]
- Transoral for Group A: "Transoral odontoidectomy and resection of the superior half or third of the C2 body was a gratifying surgical procedure ... Following this surgery, there was a clinical recovery in all the patients in this group." (earlier approach) [goel p.7]; "anterior transoral decompressive surgery is relevant in Group A patients; although in our current evaluation we recommend craniovertebral realignment in these patients." [goel p.7]
- Complications: "Opening of the dura ... increased risk of cerebrospinal fluid fistula." [goel p.11] "no postoperative vascular, neurological or infective complications" (Group A series) [goel p.9]; "No patient complained of numbness in the suboccipital region, but on a leading question agreed to have a patch of suboccipital numbness." [goel p.9]
- Limitation noted by author: "Stainless steel plates, non-locking variety of screws and custom-made spacers were used due to the higher costs of branded material." [goel p.10-11]; effect on syrinx could not be confirmed (text garbled: "the effect on syringomyelia not be confirmed" [goel p.10]).
- Natural history: "Even the natural history has not been clearly elucidated in the literature." [goel p.2, pr. 236]

- "Neck pain was a major presenting symptom in 77% of patients." [goel p.6]
