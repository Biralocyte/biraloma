# Facts: sutton_dahnert (S = Sutton PDF page; D = Dahnert PDF page; cited as [sutton_dahnert p.N] with S/D noted)
Note: Sutton states no modality-specific norms except where quoted. Dahnert entries come from interleaved text, checked against p.4 image.

## Anatomy & ligaments
- "The section of somite that forms bone is the sclerotome. Any abnormality in the formation of the sclerotomes or in communication between the sclerotomes that will ultimately form the upper cervical spine and basilar skull can result in a craniovertebral junction abnormality." [sutton_dahnert p.8, Sutton]
- Dahnert shows a diagram "Primary and Secondary Ossification Centers" for axis and atlas (labelled "secondary centers"); no text values. [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- No ligament anatomy text in either excerpt (only "congenital insufficiency of transverse ligament" and "laxity of transverse ligament"). 

## Imaging technique
- "The osseous features of craniovertebral junction abnormalities can often be identified on radiograph and are readily apparent on multiplanar CT. However, the soft-tissue structures, including the spinal cord and ligaments, are best evaluated by MRI." [sutton_dahnert p.9, Sutton]
- Basilar invagination "is visible on plain films, CT (Fig. 79.24), and MRI." [sutton_dahnert p.10, Sutton]
- Chamberlain and McGregor "anatomic lines drawn on the lateral view" [sutton_dahnert p.10, Sutton]
- Predental/retrodental space "measurement along midplane of atlas on lateral view" [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- "absence of arch-canal line (LAT view)"; posterior arch rachischisis "superimposed on odontoid process / axis body simulating a fracture (open-mouth odontoid view)" [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Occipital condyle hypoplasia measured "On anteroposterior views" [sutton_dahnert p.11, Sutton]

## Craniometry
- Basal (base of skull) angle: "the angle between the central part of the anterior fossa and the clivus (basal angle) is increased to 142° or more" = platybasia; "often has little if any clinical significance". Landmarks: "drawing a line from the nasion to the center of the pituitary fossa and another line from the pituitary fossa to the anterior edge of the foramen magnum". Modality: lateral/sagittal (diagram; example on CT). [sutton_dahnert p.9-10, Sutton]
- Welcher basal angle: "Welcher basal angle = sphenoid angle > 140–145°" (platybasia). [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Clivus-canal (craniovertebral) angle: "craniovertebral = clivus-canal angle becomes acute (< 150°)" in platybasia. Modality not stated. [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Clivus-canal angle "typically decreased" in basiocciput hypoplasia and in atlanto-occipital assimilation. [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Chamberlain line (Sutton): "extends from the posterior edge of the hard palate to the tip of the opisthion"; basilar invagination if "the dens extending more than 5 mm above the palato-occipital line (Chamberlain)". Lateral view. [sutton_dahnert p.10, Sutton]
- Chamberlain line (Dahnert): "(= line between hard palate + opisthion)"; basilar impression: "tip of odontoid process ... projects > 5 mm above Chamberlain line". [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- McGregor line (Sutton): "extends from the posterior edge of the hard palate to the most caudal aspect of the occipital bone"; also "palatosuboccipital line ... drawn from the bony palate to the tangency plane beneath the occipital bone"; abnormal "7 mm above the palatosuboccipital line (McGregor)". Lateral view; example on sagittal CT. [sutton_dahnert p.10, Sutton]
- Caveat: "These however may be abnormal in the presence of other insignificant anomalies, such as a congenitally short clivus and therefore a prominent slope of the foramen magnum." [sutton_dahnert p.10, Sutton]
- Bimastoid line (Dahnert): "tip of odontoid > 10 mm above bimastoid line" in condylar hypoplasia/basilar invagination. Modality not stated. [sutton_dahnert p.3, Dahnert]
- Atlanto-occipital joint axis angle: Sutton: "On anteroposterior views, the angle between two intersecting lines drawn through the atlanto-occipital joints exceeds the normal angle upper limit of 127°" (occipital condyle hypoplasia). Dahnert: "widening of atlanto-occipital joint axis angle" (value not given). [sutton_dahnert p.11 Sutton; p.3 Dahnert]
- Predental space (anterior atlantoaxial subluxation): "distance between dens + anterior arch of C1 (measurement along midplane of atlas on lateral view): (a) predental space: > 2.5 mm > 4.5 mm (in children)". [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Retrodental space: "(b) retrodental space: < 18 mm" (abnormal). Landmarks not given. [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Bowstring: "bowstring deformity of cervicomedullary junction" (platybasia). [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Not present in either excerpt: Wackenheim, Ranawat, Redlund-Johnell, Powers, BDI/BAI, clivo-axial angle by name, Klaus height, Welcher beyond above, Taylor-Chamberlain-Wackenheim.

## Congenital/developmental
- "Craniovertebral junction abnormalities ... can be categorized as congenital or acquired." Syndromic associations listed (see below). [sutton_dahnert p.8, Sutton]
- Basilar invagination (Sutton): "the normal slope of the posterior fossa down to the foramen magnum is reversed and the odontoid process extends upward through the skull base". [sutton_dahnert p.10, Sutton]
- Basilar invagination (Dahnert): "congenital primary developmental anomaly with abnormally high position of vertebral column prolapsing into skull base ! bone softening"; "Associated with: Chiari malformation, syringohydromyelia in 25–35%"; "Cause: mnemonic: MACKO - Mucopolysaccharidosis, Achondroplasia, Cleidocranial dysostosis, Klippel-Feil syndrome, Osteogenesis imperfecta"; "limitation in range of motion of CVJ". [sutton_dahnert p.3, Dahnert]
- Dahnert "abnormal craniometry" list: "Condylus tertius = ossicle at distal end of clivus ... pseudojoint with odontoid process / anterior arch of C1"; "Condylar hypoplasia: lateral masses of atlas may be fused to condyles; violation of Chamberlain line; widening of atlanto-occipital joint axis angle; tip of odontoid > 10 mm above bimastoid line"; "Basiocciput hypoplasia: shortening of clivus; violation of Chamberlain line; clivus-canal angle typically decreased". [sutton_dahnert p.3-4, Dahnert]
- Basilar impression: "acquired form of basilar invagination with bulging of C-spine and foramen magnum into cranial cavity with normal bone"; Cause "mnemonic: HORRIFic P - Hyperparathyroidism, Osteomalacia, Rickets, Rheumatoid arthritis, Infection of skull base, Fibrous dysplasia, Paget disease". [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Platybasia (Dahnert): "anthropometric term referring to flattening of skull base"; "May be associated with: basilar invagination"; "cord symptoms". [sutton_dahnert p.4]
- Occipital condyle hypoplasia: "underdevelopment of one or both occipital condyles." [sutton_dahnert p.11, Sutton]
- Atlanto-occipital assimilation (Sutton): "congenital fusion of the occipital base and the atlas (Fig. 79.25). Fusion of C3 and C2 vertebrae is often associated. If the dens is posteriorly displaced as a consequence of the osseous fusion, the patient is at risk for myelopathy. Chiari I malformations and basilar invagination are associated with atlanto-occipital assimilation." [sutton_dahnert p.11]
- Atlanto-occipital assimilation (Dahnert): "complete / partial failure of segmentation between skull + 1st cervical vertebra; violation of Chamberlain line; clivus-canal angle decreased; May be associated with: fusion of C2 + C3; Cx: atlantoaxial subluxation (50%); sudden death"; "elevation of posterior arch of C1"; "C-spine + foramen magnum bulge into cranial cavity". [sutton_dahnert p.4]
- "Occipitalization of atlas: 0.75% of population; fusion of basion + anterior arch of atlas" [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Os odontoideum (Sutton): "an ossicle superior to the dens, which becomes significant when it carries the whole of the articular surface, with the anterior arch of the atlas being then liable to subluxation with relatively minor trauma"; "the odontoid itself is relatively short, and often maintains its usual superiorly convex corticated upper border, which helps distinguish it from an acute odontoid fracture. The articulation between the os odontoideum and the dens is also above the usual site of the fracture through the dens." "isolated or associated with others, including atlanto-axial assimilation." [sutton_dahnert p.12]
- Os odontoideum (Dahnert): "independent os cephalad to axis body in location of odontoid process; absence of odontoid process; anterior arch of atlas hypertrophic + situated too far posterior in relation to axis body; Cx: atlantoaxial instability; DDx: type 2 odontoid fracture (uncorticated margin)". [sutton_dahnert p.4]
- Persistent ossiculum terminale = Bergman ossicle: "unfused odontoid process > 12 years of age; DDx: type 1 odontoid fracture". [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Odontoid hypoplasia/aplasia (Sutton): "represents a small or absent odontoid process. In aplasia, the soft-tissue structures such as ligaments may be absent on CT and MRI." Dahnert: "Odontoid aplasia (extremely rare)". [sutton_dahnert p.13 Sutton; p.4 Dahnert]
- Atlas anomalies (Dahnert): "Posterior atlas arch rachischisis (4%); Location: midline (97%); lateral through sulcus of vertebral artery (3%)"; "Total aplasia of posterior atlas arch"; "Keller-type aplasia with persistence of posterior tubercle"; "Isolated anterior arch rachischisis (0.1%)"; "Split atlas = anterior + posterior arch rachischisis"; anterior arch anomaly with "plump rounded anterior arch overlapping the odontoid process making identification of predental space impossible (LAT view)"; "duplicated anterior margins (LAT view)". [sutton_dahnert p.4]
- Dahnert CVJ anomaly mnemonic "PF ROACH": Paget, Fibrous dysplasia, Rheumatoid arthritis/Rickets, Osteogenesis imperfecta/Osteomalacia, Achondroplasia, Cleidocranial dysostosis/Chiari malformation, Hyperparathyroidism. [sutton_dahnert p.3]
- Spina bifida occulta: "estimated to be present in 40% of people [children], which decreases with age to approximately 7% in adults" (original: "quite common in childhood, estimated to be present in 40% of people"). [sutton_dahnert p.13, Sutton]

## Trauma & classifications
- Trauma listed as acquired CVJ cause; "(very rare without odontoid fracture)" for atlantoaxial subluxation. [sutton_dahnert p.8 Sutton; p.4 Dahnert]
- Os odontoideum vs fracture: type 2 odontoid fracture has "uncorticated margin"; type 1 fracture DDx for ossiculum terminale. Sutton: corticated, superiorly convex upper border. [sutton_dahnert p.4, p.12]
- NOT in excerpts: Anderson-D'Alonzo, Traynelis, Jefferson/Landells, Levine-Edwards, Effendi, Anderson-Montesano, Tuli, Fielding, Goel, Arnold-Chiari grading.

## Rheumatoid/inflammatory
- "Acquired craniovertebral junction anomalies may be related to traumatic injury or various arthritides or arthropathies, such as degenerative disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease." [sutton_dahnert p.8-9, Sutton]
- Odontoid erosion "mnemonic: P LARD - Psoriasis, Lupus erythematosus, Ankylosing spondylitis, Rheumatoid arthritis, Down syndrome". [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Anterior atlantoaxial subluxation arthritis causes "due to laxity of transverse ligament or erosion of dens": rheumatoid, psoriatic, Reiter, ankylosing spondylitis, SLE; "rare: in gout + CPPD". "JAP LARD": Juvenile rheumatoid arthritis, Ankylosing spondylitis, Psoriatic arthritis, Lupus erythematosus, ... [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert; list truncated at page end]
- Inflammatory process (Grisel-type): "pharyngeal infection in childhood, retropharyngeal abscess, coryza, otitis media, mastoiditis, cervical adenitis, parotitis, alveolar abscess; dislocation 8–10 days after onset of symptoms". [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Basilar impression causes include rheumatoid arthritis. [p.4, Dahnert]

## Infection incl TB
- "Other causes include neoplasm and infection, including skull base osteomyelitis." [sutton_dahnert p.9, Sutton]
- "Infection of skull base" in HORRIFic P (basilar impression). [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Sutton reference list has spinal infection refs ([21] pyogenic/tuberculous/brucellar vertebral osteomyelitis; [26] DWI claw sign) but no CVJ-specific TB text.

## Neoplasm
- Metastatic spinal column report checklist: "1. whether epidural neoplasm is present, 2. whether there is mass effect on the thecal sac, spinal cord contact, and/or spinal cord deformity, 3. whether there is myelographic block, and 4. whether there is signal abnormality within the cord." "Malignant spinal cord or cauda equina compression may require urgent treatment, whether surgical decompression or radiation." [sutton_dahnert p.14, Sutton]
- "noncontrast CT may clarify indeterminate findings on MRI, and bone scintigraphy may help determine malignant likelihood." [p.14]

## Syndromic (Down, Morquio, achondroplasia etc.)
- "Some uncommon entities are associated with childhood syndromes (e.g., Conradi, Morquio, Klippel-Feil, Down, Goldenhar), Paget's disease, and osteogenesis imperfecta." [sutton_dahnert p.8, Sutton]
- Atlantoaxial subluxation congenital causes: "Down syndrome (20%)", "Morquio syndrome", "Bone dysplasia", "Os odontoideum / aplasia of dens", "Congenital insufficiency of transverse ligament". [sutton_dahnert p.4, Dahnert]
- Basilar invagination causes MACKO include mucopolysaccharidosis, achondroplasia, cleidocranial dysostosis, Klippel-Feil, osteogenesis imperfecta. [p.3, Dahnert]
- "E. MARFAN DISEASE" listed as cause of atlantoaxial subluxation. [p.4, Dahnert]

## Degenerative/other
- Degenerative disease and CPPD listed as acquired causes. [sutton_dahnert p.8-9, Sutton]
- Degenerative spine reporting (preceding text): "describing the cause of the narrowing ... often changes the treatment approach"; "progressive slippage is an indication for surgical fusion" (spondylolysis). [sutton_dahnert p.7, Sutton]

## Instability & management / surgical decision points
- Dahnert CVJ anomaly "Cx: compression of brainstem (a) aqueductal stenosis, hydrocephalus (b) cord edema, syrinx" [arrows lost in text layer]; "Rx: surgical decompression of posterior cranial fossa / foramen magnum (posterior approach); resection of odontoid (anterior approach)". [sutton_dahnert p.3, Dahnert]
- Os odontoideum "Cx: atlantoaxial instability". Atlanto-occipital assimilation Cx "atlantoaxial subluxation (50%); sudden death". [p.4, Dahnert]
- Sutton: "If the dens is posteriorly displaced as a consequence of the osseous fusion, the patient is at risk for myelopathy." [p.11]
- Fig 79.24 B shows "artifacts related to previous occipitocervical fusion posteriorly" (no management text). [p.10]
- Platybasia "often has little if any clinical significance". [p.9]

## Urgent findings
- Clinical presentation: "neck pain and occipital headache. Brainstem or spinal cord compression may result in cranial nerve deficits, gait abnormalities, vertigo, weakness, sensory deficits, and other symptoms of myelopathy." [sutton_dahnert p.8, Sutton]
- "sudden death" with atlanto-occipital assimilation (Dahnert p.4).
- "Chiari I malformation, with cerebellar tonsillar descent" and "Chiari malformation, syringohydromyelia in 25-35%" with basilar invagination.
