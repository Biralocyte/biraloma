## Anatomy & ligaments
- "The craniovertebral junction (CVJ) anatomically denotes the occiput, the first (atlas) and second cervical (axis) vertebral segments, their articulations and connecting ligaments." [aiims p.5]
- "The occiput which surrounds the foramen magnum consists of three parts: (1) The squamosal portion (supra-occiput) situated in the dorsal aspect of foramen magnum, (2) The basi-occiput or clival portion located anterior to the foramen magnum, and (3) The condylar part (exo-occiput) that joins the clival parts and the squamosal." [aiims p.5]
- "The atlantoaxial complex is unique among the intervertebral articulations on account of the horizontal orientation of its lateral facet joints, pivot-like atlantodental articulation and special ligamentous support (alar ligaments and transverse or cruciate ligament)." [aiims p.7]
- "The occipito-atlantoaxial ligaments are arranged in four layers. The most anterior layer is the superior extension of anterior longitudinal ligament known as anterior atlanto- occipital membrane that it attaches to the anterior arch of the atlas. The second layer is formed by the alar ligament and the apical ligament. The paired alar ligaments connect the odontoid with the lateral masses and the occipital condyles. The small apical ligament extends from the tip of the odontoid to the foramen magnum. The cruciate ligament forms the third layer and is seen immediately dorsal to the odontoid process. The transverse portion of the cruciate ligament or the transverse ligament connects both lateral masses of the atlas and passes dorsal to the odontoid, keeping it pressed forward against the anterior arch of the atlas. The most posterior layer is formed by the tectorial membrane which is a superior continuation of the posterior longitudinal ligament." [aiims p.7]
- "There is no vascular communication between the base of dens and body of axis. This is important in understanding the etiology of Os odontoideum and formation of a sequestrum with type II odontoid fracture." [aiims p.7]
- Embryology: "The bones of the CVJ are developed from four occipital sclerotomes and the first two cervical sclerotomes. The first two occipital sclerotomes form the basiocciput. The third occipital sclerotome forms the exoccipital bone that forms jugular tubercles. The key sclerotome is the fourth occipital sclerotome termed the pro-atlas. It forms the anterior tubercle of the clivus, the apical cap of the dens, anterior margin of the foramen magnum, occipital condyles, lateral atlantal masses and the superior portion of the posterior arch of the atlas. The first spinal sclerotome forms the anterior arch of atlas, posterior and inferior portion of the C1 arch, odontoid process. The second cervical sclerotome forms the rest of the axis vertebra (Fig. 3)." [aiims p.7]
- "Lymphatic drainage is primarily into the retropharyngeal lymph nodes, and then into the upper jugular deep cervical chain. Thus, a retrograde infection may affect the synovial lining of the CVJ complex." [aiims p.8]
- Biomechanics: "Nodding or yes-yes movements occur at the atlanto-occipital joints. Rotatory or no-no movements occur at atlantoaxial joints" ; "The average range of this motion at the occipitoatlantal joint is 13-15°. An additional 10° of motion occurs in the atlantoaxial articulation." ; "Atlantoaxial joint rotation varies from 37° to 42°." [aiims p.9]
- "Flexion is limited by the tectorial membrane and by contact between the dens and occipital basion. Extension is restricted by the stretching of tectorial membrane and by bony contact between opisthion and posterior arch of atlas." [aiims p.9]
- "In the normal adult less than 3 mm of anteroposterior translation occurs between the dens and anterior ring of atlas. This measurement may be up to 5 mm in children younger than 8 years of age. If the transverse component of cruciate ligament ruptures with intact alar ligament, up to 5 mm of displacement may occur." [aiims p.9]
- "The odontoid tip normally lies approximately at the level of the superior margin of the anterior arch of atlas." [aiims p.19]

## Imaging technique
- "These measurements were initially defined on plain X-rays but are now extrapolated to NCCT saggital and coronal images." [aiims p.10]
- Radiography: "Translateral view of skull including the upper cervical spine (neutral, flexion and extension). These give the points of references for various radiological base lines and angles in assessing atlanto-odontoid distance, anomalies of dens and arches of atlas and axis vertebrae and the soft tissues of neck." "Open mouth view-additionally detects dens anomalies and its lateral displacement." [aiims p.14]
- "Conventional tomography (AP and lateral): It defines the CVJ relationship and dimensions (rarely done)." [aiims p.15]
- "Noncontrast CT: Axial sections show arches of atlas, lateral masses, atlanto-odontoid distance, position of dens and canal diameters. Structures within the canal are indistinguishable without the use of subarachnoid contrast. CT helps to make definitive diagnosis of atlantoaxial rotatory fixation and spatial appreciation of bony fragments in relation to foramen magnum." [aiims p.15]
- "Three-dimensional CT and reconstruction images: It helps in understanding the spatial relationships of complex skeletal and traumatic pathology and makes it possible to make a definitive and comprehensive radiographic diagnosis, without additional radiation exposure or significant modification of scanning technique. Little literature is available about its potentials in evaluating CVJ." [aiims p.15]
- "Conventional MRI: It provides multiplanar images and is especially helpful in evaluating the brainstem, spinal cord, cerebellar tonsils, syringohydromyelia, cervicomedullary kink and hydrocephalus, etc." [aiims p.15]
- Dynamic MRI: "A study conducted in this institute on the role of dynamic MRI in evaluating the CV-Junction abnormalities in 25 patients revealed the diagnosis of mobile AAD, dynamic nature of spinal canal narrowing and variability in cord compression with changing position of the neck. In neutral position, 81% patients had some degree of canal narrowing out of which 16% patients showed increase in canal narrowing on flexion with half of them having direct cord compression which was not detected in neutral position. Thus, dynamic MRI was able to detect cases where surgical intervention was needed as the cord was being compressed in one of the physiological positions." [aiims p.15]
- "The knowledge of dynamic nature of cord compression is important in planning the management of these abnormalities." [aiims p.15]
- "A combination of MRI and either standard radiography or CT is ideal in evaluating most of the CVJ anomalies. In all techniques of investigations, dynamic flexion and extension studies are essential to assess the stability and angular osseous relationships and to provide information related to their reducibility and position of fixation." [aiims p.16]
- Institutional comparison: "the most common radiological abnormality was bony atlas anomalies (86.7%) followed by AAD (80%) and multiple anomalies (50%). Three-dimensional CT was found either equivalent or superior to plain X-rays and tomograms except in the assessment of foramen magnum dimensions." [aiims p.16]
- Summary: "MR in association with either plain X-rays or 3D CT would be ideal in the evaluation of CVJ anomalies." [aiims p.34]
- Trauma imaging: "NCCT is now the mainstay in imaging of the trauma for bone and high resolution MRI for the soft tissue." "CT with sagittal and coronal reformatted images permits optimal detection and evaluation of fracture and subluxation. The atlanto-occipital and atlantoaxial articulations should be congruent on both coronal and sagittal reformatted images." [aiims p.27]
- "Magnetic resonance imaging of the cervical spine with fat-suppressed gradient-echo T2W or STIR sequences can display an increased signal intensity in the atlantoaxial and atlanto-occipital joints, craniocervical ligaments, prevertebral soft tissues, and the spinal cord (Figs. 19A and B). Axial gradient-echo MRIs may be prominently useful in assessing the integrity of the transverse atlantal ligament." [aiims p.27]
- Phase-contrast: "researchers have used phase-contrast line MRI to calculate and study CSF flow across Foramen Magnum. During cardiac systole the CSF goes down the spinal canal across the F Magnum and during diastole the reverse happens." [aiims p.31]

## Craniometry
General: "Craniometry of the CVJ uses a series of lines, planes and angles to define the normal anatomic relationships of the CVJ (Figs. 5A and B). These measurements were initially defined on plain X-rays but are now extrapolated to NCCT saggital and coronal images." [aiims p.10]
Table 1 (lateral view; table printed on p12-13; source gives values on plain radiograph; modality not given per line). Columns: definition / normal / implication.
- Wackenheim's line (Clivus canal line): "Line drawn along clivus into cervical canal"; normal "Odontoid tip is ventral and tangential to this line"; implication "Odontoid process transects the line in basilar invagination or forward position of skull". [aiims p.12]
- Chamberlain's line (Palato-occipital line): "Joins posterior pole of hard palate to opisthion"; normal "Tip of dens 1 + 3.6 mm below this line" (sic); implication "Odontoid process bisects the line in basilar invagination". [aiims p.12]
- McRae's line (Foramen magnum line): "Joins anterior and posterior edges of foramen magnum (Basion to opisthion)"; normal "Tip of dens does not exceed this line"; implication "When effective sagittal diameter is <20 mm, neurological symptoms occur (Foramen magnum stenosis)". [aiims p.12]
- McGregor's line (Basal line): "Hard plate to the lowest point of occipital bone"; normal "Tip of dens should not exceed 5 mm above the line"; implication "Line position varies with flexion + extension hence not important". [aiims p.12]
- Height index of Klauss: "Distance between tip of dens and tuberculum torcula line"; normal "40-41 mm"; implication "<30 mm seen in basilar invagination". [aiims p.12]
- Spinolamellar line (Spinous interlaminar line): "Line drawn from interoccipital ridge above and down along the fused spinous process of C2 and C3"; normal "Should intersect posterior arch of atlas"; implication "If atlas is fused, posterior arch is anterior to the line, posterior compression of spinal cord may occur". [aiims p.12]
- Boogard's line: "Joins nasion to opisthion"; normal "Basion below this line"; implication "Altered in basilar impression". [aiims p.12]
- Atlanto temporo-mandibular index: "Joins superior edge of anterior arch of atlas to the highest point of temporomandibular interspace"; normal "30 + 9 mm" (sic); implication "<10 mm in basilar invagination". [aiims p.12]
- Basion odontoid index (Whooley): "Joins basion to the trip [sic] of odontoid process"; normal "5 mm in adults up to 10 mm in children (in neutral position)"; implication "Measurements altered in both flexion and extension". [aiims p.12-13]
- Basal angle: "Angle between the lines drawn from nasion to tuberculum sellae and tuberculum sellae to basion"; normal "124-142°"; implication "Widening is called platy-basia". [aiims p.13]
- Bull's angle (Atlantopalatine angle): "Angle formed by joining the hard palate posteriorly and the line joining mid points of anterior and posterior arches of atlas"; normal "<10°"; implication ">13° in basilar invagination (BI)  10-13° : tendency to BI". [aiims p.13]
- Boogard's angle: "Angle between McRae's line and line drawn from dorsum sellae to basion along the plane of clivus"; normal "122°"; implication ">135° in basilar invagination". [aiims p.13]
Table 2 (AP view; Table 2 on p14):
- Fishgold digastric line (Biventer line): "Joins the fossae for digastric muscles on undersurface of skull (Just medial to mastoid process)"; normal "Dens tip should not project above this line. Central axis of dens should be perpendi cular to the line"; implication "Corresponds to McRae's line on lateral view: may be oblique in unilateral condylar hypoplasia: oblique odontoid suggest paramedian abnormality". [aiims p.14]
- Fishgold bimastoid line: "Line connecting tips of mastoid process"; normal "Runs across atlantooccipital joints, line is 10 mm below digastric line"; implication "Odontoid tip may be the line" (sic, truncated). [aiims p.14]
- Schmidt-Fischer angle: "Angle of axes of atlanto-occipital joints"; normal "124-127° should be measured in plane dens on tomography"; implication "Angle is wider in condylar hypoplasia". [aiims p.14]
Other measurements in text:
- Atlas-dens interval in AAI (radiographs in flexion/extension): "An atlas dens interval of more than 4 mm in children and more than 3 mm in adults is diagnostic. If more than 5 mm motion of anterior atlantoaxial interval is present, conclusive evidence of instability exists." [aiims p.25]
- Trauma: "Increased atlantoaxial distance, which should be less than 4 mm in children under 9 years of age and less than 3 mm in adults, points to injury of the transverse ligament." [aiims p.27] (Note: p9 gives 'up to 5 mm in children younger than 8 years'; p25 '>4 mm in children'; p27 '<4 mm under 9 years' - age thresholds differ across the chapter.)
- "An abnormal distance between the dens or posterior axial line and the basion suggests failure or insufficiency of the alar ligaments, tectorial membrane, or both." [aiims p.27] (no numeric cut-off given)
- "On the open-mouth view of the CVJ, the lateral masses of C1 should align exactly with the lateral margins of C2 vertebra." [aiims p.27]
- Clivus-canal angle: "clivus is short and clivus-canal angle is typically less than 150°" (basilar invagination with basi-occiput hypoplasia, Fig. 8). [aiims p.19]
- Platybasia (Fig. 9): "clivus-canal angle becomes acute (<150°), Welcher basal angle >140°." [aiims p.20]
- Os terminale: "This apical segment is usually about 12 mm in length, but can be very small." [aiims p.22]
- Fig. 9 images display on-screen angles 146.823° (sagittal CT) and 131.290° (coronal CT); caption does not identify them (see figs csv).

## Congenital/developmental
- Classification of CVJ anomalies (p10-13): Congenital: "Malformations of the occipital sclerotomes including proatlas: Clivus segmentation anomalies, Proatlas remnants around foramen magnum, Condylar hypoplasia, Assimilation of atlas"; "Malformation of atlas: Assimilation of atlas, Atlantoaxial fusion, Aplasia of atlas arches"; "Malformation of axis: Irregular atlantoaxial segmentation, Dens dysplasias"; "Ossiculum terminale persistens"; "Os odontoideum"; "Hypoplasia-aplasia: Segmentation failure of C2-C3". Developmental/acquired: "Abnormalities at the foramen magnum. Foraminal stenosis (e.g. achondroplasia)"; "Secondary basilar invagination (e.g. Osteogenesis imperfecta, Hadju-Cheney, Paget's disease, osteomalacia, rickets, rheumatoid arthritis)"; "Atlantoaxial instability: Down syndrome; Ehlers-Danlos syndrome; Errors of metabolism: Morquio's, Hurlers; Trauma; Infection: Tuberculosis; Inflammatory: Rheumatoid arthritis, Reiter's syndrome; Tumor: Osteoblastoma, eosinophilic granuloma, chordoma, neurofibromatosis, metastasis"; "Miscellaneous, e.g. Conradi-Hünermann syndrome, fetal warfarin syndrome, fibrous dysplasia"; "Spontaneous atlantoaxial rotatory subluxation: Grisel syndrome". [aiims p.10, 13]
- Epidemiology (South Asia): "Craniovertebral junction anomalies are seen all over the world, but are common in our country." "In a study over a period of 12 years, they were responsible for 3.03% of total admissions to the neurology wards." "They were the single major cause of cervical cord compression, accounting for nearly half the cases. Therefore, in every patient presenting with high cervical cord compression in our country, the diagnosis of CVJ anomalies must be excluded. Majority of patients are between 11 and 40 years, although the defect is present since birth. There is a male preponderance. Onset is usually gradual. The most common precipitating factor is in the form of trivial trauma to the neck either by a fall or by carrying heavy loads at the back of head. Recurrent throat infection and fever may lead to hyperemia and laxity of ligament causing dislocations." [aiims p.13]
- "A study of 82 patients with CVJ anomalies in our institute showed congenital atlantoaxial dislocation (AAD) to be the most common (56%) anomaly followed by multiple anomalies (48.8%) the common combinations being AAD with occipitalization of C1, basilar invagination with Chiari malformation and basilar invagination with occipitalization of C1." [aiims p.14]
- "In a recent study of 70 patients with bony CVJ anomalies in our institute, the most common presenting symptom was cervical pain and stiffness (77%) and majority of the patients were in second decade. The common neurological sign was involvement of pyramidal tract (70%)." [aiims p.14]
- Clinical: "Myelopathy is the most common neurological deficit." "Lower cranial nerve dysfunction is seen in 20% of children with basilar invagination. Vascular symptoms occur in 15% patients. Neck pain is present in 85% patients typically described as originating in the suboccipital area with radiation to the vertex." [aiims p.10]
- Basilar invagination: "It is a developmental defect implying prolapse of the vertebral column into the skull base (Fig. 6). There is a distinct developmental defect of the chondrocranium, often associated with other anomalies of the notochord in the region of the CVJ such as occipitalization of atlas (Figs. 7A to E) and Klippel-Feil syndrome. Basilar invagination is also associated with anomalies of development of the epicaudal neuroaxis such as Chiari malformations, syringobulbia and syringomyelia in about 25-30% of cases." [aiims p.16]
- "Two types of basilar invagination are recognized. In the anterior variety, there is shortening of the basiocciput and the clivus is short and horizontally oriented known as Basi-occiput hypoplasia (Fig. 8). In the paramedian variety, there is hypoplasia of the exoccipital bones and dorsal displacement of the clivus, which is otherwise of normal length." [aiims p.16]
- Basilar impression: "refers to secondary or acquired forms of basilar invagination due to softening of the bone as occurs in hyperparathyroidism, rickets, osteomalacia, Paget's disease, Hurler's syndrome, osteogenesis imperfecta, etc. Traumatic basilar impression has also been reported." "In this anomaly, all three parts of the occipital bone (basiocciput, exoccipital bone, and supraoccipital bone) are deformed. There is abnormal rostral location of clivus-atlas-odontoid complex within the foramen magnum." [aiims p.17]
- "Platybasia refers only to an abnormally obtuse basal angle. It is not a measure of basilar invagination, although it may be associated with invagination (Figs. 9A and B). There are no symptoms or signs associated with platybasia alone." [aiims p.17]
- Assimilation of atlas: "represents the most cephalic blocked vertebra encountered in the spine. It is defined as a failure of segmentation between the atlas and skull and occurs in 0.25% of the population. It may be bilateral, unilateral, segmental, or focal (Figs. 10A and B). In most instances, atlas assimilation occurs in association with other abnormalities such as basilar invagination and Klippel-Feil syndrome. Associated congenital anomalies present in 20% of patients, include incomplete clefting of the nasal cartilage, cleft palate, congenital external ear deformities, cervical ribs hypo-spadiasis and urinary tract anomalies." [aiims p.17]
- "A combination of assimilation of the atlas and segmentation failures between the second and third cervical vertebrae results in progressive laxity of the atlantoaxial joint with development of luxation between the atlas and axis in childhood in about 50% of cases. The progressive proliferation of granulation tissue due to instability leads to irreducible compression." [aiims p.17]
- Atlas arch anomalies: "The most common anomaly in this group is posterior rachischisis, which is present in 4% of the autopsy specimens of adults. Majority of these are midline in location (97%). Posterior arch rachischisis has been reported in 70% of children with myelomeningoceles. It may be associated with atlantoaxial instability." [aiims p.17]
- "Anterior arch rachischisis is very rare and occurs only in 0.1% adult autopsy specimens. It is almost invariably encountered with posterior rachischisis, in case of which the term "split atlas" is applied (Figs. 11A and B)." [aiims p.19]
- Split atlas vs Jefferson: "Malformations of atlas may be confused with Jefferson fracture. CT may help in differentiating the two entities. Margins of developmental cleft are smooth with an intact cortical edge with no abnormal soft tissue, whereas fractures have jagged edges or are comminuted and generally associated with soft tissue swelling." [aiims p.19]
- Dens dysplasia: "Hypoplasia of the odontoid process (Figs. 12A to C) predisposes to atlantoaxial instability, injuries and rheumatoid complications. A hypertrophic odontoid may be viewed as manifestations of the occipital vertebra. Significant vascular compromise on stretching and distortion of the vertebral artery may occur with odontoid dysplasias." [aiims p.19]
- Os terminale: "It refers to the nonfused terminal part of the odontoid derived from the centrum of the IVth occipital sclerotome... In case of disruption at the interface and if AAD occurs, the remaining part of the odontoid may compress the cervicomedullary region." [aiims p.22]
- Os odontoideum: "This denotes an independent bone seen cranial to the axis in place of the dens, which is hypoplastic. Its most common location is in the position of the normal odontoid tip or near the base of the occiput in the area of foramen magnum, where it may fuse with the clivus. This leads to incompetence of the cruciate ligaments and atlantoaxial instability. Hypertrophy of the anterior arch of C1 is often associated with Os odontoideum (Figs. 13A to D). There are two hypotheses postulated congenital and traumatic in the causation of Os odontoideum. The weight of the current evidence now points to a traumatic etiology in the majority of cases of Os odontoideum." [aiims p.22-23]
- "Two types of Os odontoideum are described: Dystopic and Orthotopic. In the orthotopic variety, the Os lies in the position of normal dens and moves in unison with the atlas and axis. In the dystopic variety, the Os lies near the basion or may be fused with the occipital bone and moves in unison with the clivus. The anterior arch of the atlas is hypertrophied and the posterior arch is often hypoplastic. In the orthotopic Os odontoideum, the transverse component of the cruciate ligament is functional as there is movement in unison with atlas and axis. The incidence of Os odontoideum is increased in patients with Down's syndrome, spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia, or Morquio's syndrome." [aiims p.23]
- Os vs type II fracture: "It is important to differentiate Os odontoideum from type II odontoid fracture. CT is the best modality to distinguish these two entities. In Os odontoideum, the upper margin of the body of axis is convex and may be associated with hypertrophy of anterior arch of atlas seen as enlargement, convex posterior border and increased cortical thickness (Fig. 14A)." "In odontoid fracture, the upper margin of axis is flattened with sharp uncorticated margin and normal appearing anterior arch (Fig. 14B)." [aiims p.23-24] (Figure citations reversed relative to the Fig. 14 caption.)

## Trauma & classifications
- "Trauma can cause fractures of clivus, C-1, C-2 vertebrae either in isolation or in combinations." [aiims p.27]
- "Fractures of the tip of the dens or of the occipital condyles may be associated with alar ligament insufficiency. Subarachnoid hemorrhage and epidural hematoma may accompany injuries to the craniocervical junction." [aiims p.27]
- Odontoid fractures: "The most common bone involved in the fractures of this region is odontoid process. Conventionally, its fractures are classified into three types-Type-I, if there is avulsion of the tip (Fig. 17); Type-II, if there is fracture of the body of the odontoid process (Figs. 18A to E) and Type-III if the odontoid process is fractured along with base of C-2." [aiims p.27] (Anderson-D'Alonzo is not named in the chapter.)
- "Conventional radiography and CT findings in AAD include prevertebral soft-tissue swelling, C1-C2 dislocation or subluxation, and widening of the C1-C2 facets. MRI findings consist of prevertebral, interspinous, or nuchal ligament edema as well as facet widening or fluid, possible epidural hematoma, and increased signal intensity of the spinal cord on T2W or STIR images." [aiims p.27]
- "The various types of CVJ injuries is been discussed elsewhere in this book." [aiims p.27]
- Fig 18 caption: "cortical breach at the base of dens, more marked in flexion images"; "cord compression and cervical cord signal changes". Fig 19: "Type-II fracture showing cord contusion on T2WI and marrow edema on the STIR image." [aiims p.33, 35]

## Rheumatoid/inflammatory
- "The most common disorder in this category is rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Other disorders include psoriatic and osteoarthritis, synovial cysts and calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease (CPPD), etc." [aiims p.26]
- "In RA, cervical spine is clinically affected in 44-88% of patients. Three types of abnormalities are seen: (1) AAI (25%), (2) upward migration of odontoid process or cranial settling (8-16%) and (3) rheumatoid granulation tissue or pannus formation around the odontoid process (10-30%)." [aiims p.26]
- "In cranial settling, there is erosion and compression of lateral masses of C1, downward displacement of anterior arch with cranial rotation of posterior arch of C1 resulting in upward migration of dens. The AP diameter of the cervical canal is reduced causing compression." [aiims p.26]
- "MRI is the imaging modality of choice for the evaluation of rheumatoid pannus. The rheumatoid pannus can be of three types, i.e. hypervascular, hypovascular or fibrous types. On T2W images the hypervascular pannus is hyperintense, whereas hypovascular and fibrous pannus show intermediate and low signal intensity respectively. The hypervascular pannus and the rheumatoid joint effusion, both of which are hyperintense on T2W images can be differentiated by presence of early intense contrast enhancement in hypervascular pannus on dynamic postcontrast studies (Figs. 15A to E). The hypovascular pannus shows delayed enhancement whereas fibrous type of pannus shows no enhancement on postcontrast images (Figs. 16A to C)." [aiims p.26]
- Fig. 15: "minimally increased predental interval in extension position which is more marked in flexion position"; "predental hypointense soft tissue that is showing intense postcontrast enhancement." Fig. 16: "Chronic RA with hypointense soft tissue at the CVJ without any enhancement." [aiims p.28-29]
- CPPD: "has a predilection for anterior atlantoaxial joint. The transverse ligament is usually the site of involvement with presence of retro-odontoid soft tissue. The radiographic findings are usually subtle. The NCCT images show retro-odontoid calcifications and the MRI shows the retro-odontoid soft tissue that is hypointense on both T1W and T2W images with the disruption of transverse ligament at times." [aiims p.26]
- AAI associations: "AAI or occipitoatlantoaxial dislocations have been reported with upper respiratory tract infection, mastoiditis, partotitis, tuberculosis, gout, seronegative arthropathy, rheumatoid arthritis and following tonsillectomy, adenoidectomy, mastoidectomy and cardiac surgeries." [aiims p.25]

## Infection incl TB
- "Infectious diseases with CVJ involvement are rare and include tuberculosis and fungal infections." [aiims p.26]
- "Tuberculosis of atlantoaxial region accounts for less than 1% of all case of spinal tuberculosis. Local neck pain tenderness and restriction of neck movements are the cardinal features." [aiims p.26]
- "The disease is divided into three stages: 1. Stage I: Retropharyngeal abscess AAD/AAI Normal bony architecture; 2. Stage II: Retropharyngeal abscess ± AAD mild bony destruction; 3. Stage III: Retropharyngeal abscess ± AAD gross bony destruction." [aiims p.26-27] (Stage-I line prints as "Retropharyngeal abscess AAD/AAI"; "Normal bony architecture" is on the next page; named staging system not attributed; reference 31 is Khandelwal et al., tuberculous atlanto-axial dislocation.)
- "Myelo-CT or MR demonstrates the characteristic radiographic appearance of bony destruction of C1-C2 with increased prevertebral soft tissue." [aiims p.27]
- "Inflammation and infection producing bone destruction with or without ligamentous instability may also cause basilar impression, e.g. rheumatoid arthritis, tuberculosis, etc." [aiims p.17]

## Syndromic (Down, Morquio, achondroplasia etc.)
- Klippel-Feil: "The classic triad consists of a low posterior hairline, short neck and limitation of neck movement. The syndrome is associated with fused vertebrae (usually C2-3 and C5-6 interfaces), hemivertebra, spina bifida occulta and scoliosis. Along with the vertebral segmentation anomalies, many CVJ anomalies are often present including basilar invagination, atlanto-occipital assimilation, odontoid hypoplasia platybasia, and Chiari I malformation. Associated congenital anomalies include urogenital anomalies, otological anomalies, cleft palate, etc. Syndactyly, Sprengel's shoulder and absence of ulna are common." [aiims p.30]
- Down: "Down syndrome or trisomy 21, the most frequent of the chromosomal disorders, is frequently complicated by atlanto- occipital instability (61%) as well as atlantoaxial instability (9-30%). Other observed osseous abnormalities comprise of persistent synchondroses, posterior C1 rachischisis and Os odontoideum." [aiims p.30] Also: "The 10-20% of children with Down's syndrome have atlantoaxial instability radiologically, and only 18% of them are symptomatic." [aiims p.25] (ranges differ: 9-30% vs 10-20%.)
- Arnold-Chiari: "The Arnold-Chiari malformations (ACM), type I through III, are a group of disorders characterized by increasing degrees of hindbrain dysgenesis. Each may be associated with deformities of the CVJ." [aiims p.30]
- Chiari I: "the vermis, fourth ventricle and medulla are normal or very slightly deformed. Elongated pointed peg-like cerebellar tonsils are displaced inferiorly through the foramen magnum into the upper cervical spinal canal. A syrinx is present in 20-40% of all patients with Chiari I, which increase to 60-90% in symptomatic patients. Osseous anomalies are seen in 25% patients in the form of basilar invaginations (25-50%) Klippel-Feil anomaly (5-10%) (Figs. 20A to C) and atlanto-occipital assimilation (1-5%)." [aiims p.30-31]
- Chiari II: "herniation of the inferior portions of the vermis, fourth ventricle and medulla into the spinal canal, kinking and displacing normal structures. It is associated with small posterior fossa, low lying transverse sutures, concave clivus and petrous ridges and fenestrate falx. The other associations include myelomeningocele (95-100%), hydrocephalus (90%) syringohydromyelia (50-90%) and segmentation anomalies (<10%)." [aiims p.31]
- Chiari III: "defined as herniation of the hindbrain associated with occipital encephalocele in combination with many of the features of the Chiari II malformation. MR is the best modality to visualize the various anomalies of ACM." [aiims p.31]
- Achondroplasia: "most common of the short-limbed dwarfisms... The foramen magnum is constricted and has a distinct tear drop configuration with obliteration of the surrounding subarachnoid space. Other CVJ abnormalities include odontoid dysplasia, basiocciput hypoplasia, decrease in the basal angle and thickening of the posterior rim of the foramen magnum." [aiims p.31]
- Mucopolysaccharidoses: "Abnormalities at the CVJ are a major cause of morbidity and mortality especially in Morquio (type IV) and Hurler (type I) varieties. These include a hypoplastic/ dysplastic odontoid process, ligamentous instability, soft- tissue thickening around the dens, and compression of the cord by the posterior atlas arch. The soft-tissue mass around the dens is typically hypointense or isointense on T1W and hypointense on T2W MRIs which may represent nonossified fibrocartilage." [aiims p.31]
- Osteogenesis imperfecta: "Pathology at the CVJ is most commonly seen in type IV, with a prevalent in the range of rare to 25%. The osteoporotic bones result in repetitive microfractures causing infolding of the occipital condyles with elevation of the clivus and the posterior cranial fossa, accompanied by an upward migration of the cervical spine into the foramen magnum (basilar impression)." [aiims p.31]

## Instability & management/surgical decision points
- "Stability at atlantoaxial area is dependent on integrity of osseous, ligamentous and muscle complex. Instability of atlantoaxial joint can be documented by lateral cervical radiographs done in flexion and extension. The diagnosis of rupture of transverse ligaments and AAD is purely radiological." [aiims p.25]
- Classification of AAI: "Group I: AAI in combinations with occipitalization of atlas, fusion of C2 and C3. Odontoid is displaced posteriorly. Group II: AAI due to incompetence of odontoid process (maldevelopment) Type I: Os odontoideum; Type II: Ossiculum terminale; Type III: Agenesis of odontoid base; Type IV: Agenesis of apical segment; Type V: Agenesis of odontoid. Group III: Odontoid is seen dislocated posteriorly. There is no abnormality of C1 or odontoid." (source cites ref 27, Gupta SK et al., PGI Chandigarh; the chapter does not name it by author.) [aiims p.26]
- "Dynamic studies (Flexion and extension) are important not only for the diagnosis but also for the management, i.e. whether the instability is fixed or mobile." [aiims p.26]
- "Thus, dynamic MRI was able to detect cases where surgical intervention was needed as the cord was being compressed in one of the physiological positions." [aiims p.15]
- "The progressive proliferation of granulation tissue due to instability leads to irreducible compression." [aiims p.17]
- Flexion/extension "provide information related to their reducibility and position of fixation." [aiims p.16]
- No surgical techniques, fixation, or outcomes are described in the chapter.

## Urgent findings
- "In most cases, the symptoms of the pathologic conditions affecting the CVJ become apparent after the compromise of these neural structures... Myelopathy is the most common neurological deficit." [aiims p.9-10]
- "Increased atlantoaxial distance, which should be less than 4 mm in children under 9 years of age and less than 3 mm in adults, points to injury of the transverse ligament." [aiims p.27]
- "If more than 5 mm motion of anterior atlantoaxial interval is present, conclusive evidence of instability exists." [aiims p.25]
- "Subarachnoid hemorrhage and epidural hematoma may accompany injuries to the craniocervical junction." [aiims p.27]
- "AAI or occipitoatlantoaxial dislocations have been reported with ... following tonsillectomy, adenoidectomy, mastoidectomy and cardiac surgeries." [aiims p.25]
- "in every patient presenting with high cervical cord compression in our country, the diagnosis of CVJ anomalies must be excluded." [aiims p.13]
