## Anatomy & ligaments
- "The craniovertebral junction (CVJ) consists of the occipital bone, foramen magnum, clivus, atlas (C1), axis (C2), and ligaments of the atlanto-occipital and atlantoaxial articulations." [chen p.1]
- "The atlantoaxial joints enable head rotation about the dens axis, whereas the atlanto-occipital joints primarily allow flexion, extension, and lateral bending." [chen p.1]
- "The occipital bone is composed of the basiocciput, supraocciput, exocciput, and occipital squamosa. They are separated by cartilaginous plates called synchondroses." [chen p.1]
- "The basiocciput forms the superior half of the clivus; the squamosa forms the interparietal and supraoccipital calvaria; and the exocciput forms the lateral margins of the foramen magnum, occipital condyles, and hypoglossal canals." [chen p.1]
- "The dens (odontoid process) of C2 articulates with the anterior arch of C1 and is held in place by the transverse part (transverse ligament) of the cruciform ligament (Fig. 2), which attaches laterally to tubercles on the inner surface of C1. It is also held in place by the alar and apical ligaments." [chen p.1]
- "The superior tip of the odontoid process (ossiculum terminale) develops from the fourth occipital sclerotome. Apical, alar, and transverse ligaments ... are lacking or nonfunctional if there is no ossiculum terminale." [chen p.1]
- Embryology table (Table 1): first occipital sclerotome - basiocciput; second occipital - exoccipital centers; fourth proatlas - hypocentrum anterior tubercle clivus, centrum apical ligament and apex of dens, neural arch occipital condyles/U-shaped foramen magnum/alar and cruciform ligaments, dorsal caudal posterior arch and lateral masses of C1; first spinal - hypocentrum anterior arch of C1, centrum dens of C2; second spinal - centrum body of C2. [chen p.2] (Text p.8 also: occipital assimilation "results from failure of segmentation between the fourth occipital sclerotome and first spinal sclerotome". Text p.4: "The basiocciput is derived from the fourth occipital sclerotome" - differs from Table 1, which lists first occipital.)
- MRI of dens/C2 junction: "A transverse line of low signal with a brighter signal above and below on sagittal or coronal T1-weighted MR imaging is usually evident at the junction of the odontoid process and body of C2 (Fig. 6A) in individuals younger than 50 years of age." [chen p.1]
- "The transverse ligament appears as an oval area of signal void, posterior to the upper dens, on sagittal MR imaging (see Fig. 6) and can be visualized on axial T1-weighted MR imaging and axial CT." [chen p.1]
- "Posterior to the dens, the dura, tectorial membrane, and vertical part of the cruciform ligament are imaged as a single vertical band of low signal intensity on sagittal T1-weighted MR imaging." [chen p.1]
- "The anterior longitudinal ligament ... is distinct between the body of C2 and the anterior arch of C1." Differentiation from anterior atlanto-occipital membrane "may be difficult, especially in sagittal MR imaging". [chen p.1]
- "The posterior atlanto-occipital membrane and posterior atlantoaxial membrane are adjacent to the dura and appear as a single line of low signal on MR imaging." [chen p.1]
- "Apical and alar ligaments between the superior margin of the dens and the condyles and basion of the occipital bone, which are best identified on CT, pass through the supraodontoid space." [chen p.1, 3]
- "The supraodontoid space lies between the alar ligaments and foramen magnum and between the tectorial and atlanto-occipital membranes and is divided into a pair of symmetric halves by the apical ligament and a thin fatty membrane." [chen p.3]
- Fig. 6 caption: paramedian sagittal T1 MR (A) and high-resolution CT (B): transverse ligament (white arrow), alar ligament (black arrow), apical ligament (white arrowhead). Fig. 7: coronal high-resolution CT, bilateral alar ligaments "extending outward from the superior and lateral aspects of the dens to the medial aspect of the occipital"; apical ligament "surrounded by fat". [chen p.4]
- Clinical: "Suboccipital and neck pain (encountered in 82% of patients with CVJ lesions) is the most common sign." Brainstem/cord compression "may cause sleep apnea, dysphagia, nystagmus, dysphasia, nasal regurgitation, tongue atrophy, motor deficits, and bladder dysfunction"; vascular: "intermittent loss of consciousness, loss of visual fields, vertigo provoked by extension or rotation of the head, and basilar migraine". [chen p.3-4]

## Imaging technique
- Os odontoideum instability: "The degree of instability must be assessed by flexion and extension studies using plain radiography, polytomography, or MR imaging." [chen p.9]
- Odontoid aplasia vs os odontoideum: os fragment "may seem to lie over the C1 arch on the open-mouth odontoid view of the upper cervical spine". [chen p.9]
- Basilar invagination: "Plain radiographs allow detection of basilar invagination near the tip of the odontoid process located above Chamberlain's line ... Sagittal midline MR imaging can demonstrate shortening of the clivus (Fig. 10) and the degree of cervicomedullary compression." [chen p.4]
- Occipital condyle fracture: "They may be missed if proper tomographic imaging is not used. Diagnosis of fracture of the occipital condyle requires a high index of suspicion and high-resolution tomography." [chen p.11]
- Clivus fracture: "The diagnosis of clivus fracture from plain radiographs is difficult because of the dense petrous temporal bones. High-resolution CT scanning with multiplanar reconstruction revealed clivus fracture in 9 (0.36%) of 2500 patients with head trauma." [chen p.11]
- AOD: "High-resolution CT is necessary for accurate diagnosis (Fig. 21), and MR imaging can demonstrate the ligament and soft tissue injury well." [chen p.12]
- Jefferson: "CT may visualize bony avulsion of the transverse ligament at its insertion site. MR imaging is more helpful in assessing ligamentous damage, defining the relation of the associated herniated disk or retropulsed fragment to the spinal cord, and demonstrating cord injury." [chen p.12]
- Odontoid fracture: "MR imaging can display the relation of C1 to C2, disruption of the supporting ligaments, and spinal cord injury and compression. A fracture can be defined by the demonstration of a fracture line and associated bone marrow edema (Figs. 24-26). High-resolution CT may be better for detecting a subtle type I odontoid fracture." [chen p.13]
- Hangman's: "MR imaging is critical in defining the type of spinal cord injury, determining the extent of ligamentous injury, and detecting an associated acute herniated disk." [chen p.13]
- Neoplasm imaging role: "The role of imaging is to determine the nature and resectability of the tumors. It is important in radiologic interpretation to evaluate the tumor extent; to determine the direction of encroachment; to determine whether the lesion has a vascular or intramedullary component, such as that occurring in syringomyelia; and to evaluate the craniovertebral stability." [chen p.20]
- Meningioma: "Gadolinium-enhanced sequences help to delineate the dural attachment zone, the tumor, and its relation to neural and vascular structures. Preoperative angiography is indicated if a highly vascularized tumor is suspected and embolization is contemplated." [chen p.23]
- Epidermoid: "Diffusion-weighted imaging demonstrates restricted diffusion within epidermoid cysts, simplifying the diagnosis and aiding visualization." [chen p.25]

## Craniometry
(The source contains no dedicated craniometry section; all measurements are in the Fig. 8 caption, Fig. 9 caption, one text sentence, and the Hangman description. No modality stated for any - Fig. 8 is a line drawing, Fig. 9 shows CT and T1 MRI.)
- Welcher basal angle (platybasia angle): "formed at the intersection of the nasion-tuberculum line and the tuberculum-basion line. Its average should always be less than 140°. This angle is increased in platybasia." [chen p.6] Modality not stated (diagram). Abnormal: >140° (Fig. 9: "Welcher basal angle larger than 140°", CT sagittal and T1 MRI) [chen p.6]. Text: "Platybasia refers to an increase in the Welcher basal angle (Figs. 8 and 9). Platybasia alone does not usually cause symptoms unless it is associated with basilar invagination." [chen p.4]
- Wackenheim's (clivus baseline) line: "constructed by drawing a line along the clivus and extrapolating it inferiorly into the upper cervical spinal canal. This line should fall tangental [sic] to the posterior aspect of the tip of the odontoid process." [chen p.6] Text: "The position of the Wackenheim clivus baseline is usually normal" in basioccipital hypoplasia with invagination. [chen p.4]
- Craniovertebral (clivus-canal) angle: "The angle formed at the intersection of Wackenheim's line with a line constructed along the posterior surface of the axis body and odontoid process (craniovertebral or clivus-canal angle) normally ranges between 150° and 180°." [chen p.6] Modality not stated.
- Chamberlain's line: (landmarks not defined in text) "Normally, the anterior arch of the atlas typically lies below Chamberlain's line. The dens is 3.6 to 6.6 mm below the line." [chen p.6] Abnormal: "basilar invagination near the tip of the odontoid process located above Chamberlain's line (see Fig. 8)" on plain radiographs [chen p.4]; Fig. 9: "the dens is far above Chamberlain's line (not shown)" [chen p.6]. No numeric cut-off for invagination given. McGregor, McRae, Ranawat, Redlund-Johnell, ADI, PADI, Powers ratio: not in source.
- Basilar invagination definition: "upward herniation of the margins of the foramen magnum into the posterior fossa. Most patients with basilar invagination actually have a normal basal angle, and thus do not have associated platybasia." [chen p.4]
- Atlanto-occipital joint axis angle: condylar hypoplasia "causes basilar invagination and widening of the atlanto-occipital joint axis angle" (no value). [chen p.6]
- Hangman's type 2: "On imaging, the displacement can be more than 3 mm and associated with angulation of C2 or C3 disk space." [chen p.13] (radiograph/CT/MR not specified)
- Os odontoideum: gap "usually extends above the level of the superior articular facet of C2" (qualitative) [chen p.9].
- Odontoid aplasia: "short odontoid process extending from the C2 body to just above the lateral articular masses, with its tip lying below the level of the superior margin of the C1 anterior arch." [chen p.9]
- Age-related MRI landmark: C2 dens-body low-signal line "in individuals younger than 50 years of age" [chen p.1].

## Congenital/developmental
- Box 1 classification of CVJ disorders (Congenital malformations: occipital bone, atlas, axis/odontoid; Developmental and acquired: traumatic, inflammation/degenerative, infectious, neoplastic; Metabolic and genetic disorders). [chen p.5]
- Primary vs secondary BI: "basilar invagination can be primary or secondary. In primary basilar invagination, herniation is associated with other congenital CVJ anomalies, such as atlanto-occipital fusion, hypoplasia of C1 (Fig. 11), spina bifida of the posterior arch of C1, Klippel-Feil syndrome, Chiari malformation, and skeletal dysplasias (eg, achondroplasia). In secondary basilar invagination, the change is attributable to softening or traumatic changes of bone, such as severe osteoporosis, hyperparathyroidism, osteomalacia, rickets, renal osteodystrophy, Paget's syndrome, Hurler's syndrome, and rheumatoid arthritis (RA)." [chen p.4, 6]
- "The most common associations with basilar invagination are Chiari malformation, occipitalization of C1, and fused upper vertebrae." [chen p.6]
- Basioccipital hypoplasia: "may be mild or severe, depending on the number of occipital vertebrae affected." [chen p.4]
- Condylar hypoplasia: "the occipital condyles are underdeveloped and flat; as a result, the skull base is flattened or even ascends medially." "limits, or may even abolish, movements at the atlanto-occipital joint and may occasionally lead to compression of the vertebral artery secondary to excessive posterior gliding of the occiput in relation to the atlas (C1)." [chen p.6]
- Condylus tertius: "an ossified remnant ... at the distal end of the clivus"; "usually directly above and anterior to the arch of C1"; "may form a joint or pseudojoint with the clivus, odontoid process, or anterior arch of C1"; "may lead to limitation in the range of motion of the CVJ. An increased prevalence of os odontoideum is associated with this abnormality." Differential: Bergmann's ossicle (Fig. 13). [chen p.6-8]
- Occipital assimilation of atlas: "found in 0.14% to 0.25% of the population"; "The most common type is assimilation of the anterior arch of C1 with the anterior rim of the foramen magnum"; "On lateral plain radiographs, the C1 arches appear too high when incompletely assimilated and are not visible at all when completely assimilated." "It invariably results in basilar invagination." With Klippel-Feil "gradual loosening of the atlantodental joint with progressive atlantoaxial subluxation or dislocation may occur"; "In some instances, occipital assimilation of C1 may be associated with sudden death." [chen p.8]
- Wackenheim's classification of occipital assimilation (Table 2): Type I "Partial assimilation"; Type II "Type I + Klippel-Feil anomaly of C2 and C3"; Type III "Complete assimilation, basilar invagination, and maldevelopment of transverse ligament causing atlantoaxial dislocation"; Type IV "Complete assimilation associated with multiple malformations, including C2-to-C3 fusion, hemivertebrae, dens aplasia, tertiary condyle, and C2-to-C3 spondylolisthesis". [chen p.9] (printed "Type 1 1" for Type I +)
- C1 arch anomaly: "posterior arch rachischisis ... present in 4% of adult autopsy specimens. In 97% of cases, this defect is located midline, and in 3%, the cleft is located in the lateral aspect of C1 and through the sulcus of the vertebral artery. Anterior arch rachischisis is rare, occurring in only 0.1% of autopsy specimens." Both = "split atlas". [chen p.8]
- Odontoid aplasia/hypoplasia: "Total aplasia of the odontoid process is extremely rare." "attachments for the apical and alar ligaments are absent, predisposing to atlantoaxial dislocation." Associated with "Morquio's syndrome, spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia, and metatrophic dwarfism [sic]". [chen p.8-9]
- Persistent ossiculum terminale (Bergmann's ossicle): "failure of the terminal ossicle to fuse"; "may be confused with type I odontoid fracture ... Whether traumatic or congenital in origin, this anomaly is stable when isolated and of little clinical significance. The odontoid process is usually of normal height." [chen p.9]
- Os odontoideum: "an independent osseous structure lying cephalad to the C2 body in the area of the odontoid process. The anterior arch of C1 may appear rounded and hypertrophic and may seem to be situated too far posterior in relation to the body of C2." Type A (orthotopic): "relocation of the os odontoideum to the site of the dens and movement of the os odontoideum with C1 and C2". Type B (dystopic): "relocation ... to a site near the skull base and movement of the os odontoideum with the clivus, with which it may be fused." "cruciform ligament incompetence and atlantoaxial instability are common." Instability may "lead to substantial narrowing of the spinal canal and cord compression at the level of C1". [chen p.9] Fig. 17C: "Severe cord compression and edema can be found inside the cord after minor trauma." [chen p.10]
- Os odontoideum vs type II odontoid fracture (Table 4): history of trauma "Often present" vs "Always"; line of separation "Below superior articular facet" vs "Above superior articular facet"; "Smooth, convex superiorly, and well corticated" vs "Acute irregular and not corticated"; anterior arch of atlas "Hypertrophic and round" vs "Half-moon shaped"; associated congenital anomaly "Often present" vs "Absent". [chen p.16] (text cites "Box 1" in error, p.9)
- Klippel-Feil: "congenital fusion of the cervical spine ... failure of normal segmentation of the cervical somites between the third and eighth weeks of gestation"; localized, multisegmental, diffuse; "The classic triad (short neck, low posterior hair line, and limited range of neck movement) ... is present in less than 50% of the patients." Associations: scoliosis, kyphosis, Sprengel's deformity, renal anomalies, congenital heart disease, hearing loss, petrous bone anomalies, limb/digit anomalies. "In CVJ, high risk for early instability or degenerative disease may be seen in patients with occipitalization of the atlas accompanied by atlantoaxial fusion and odontoid hypermobility or with multisegment fusion accompanied by odontoid-atlas ring anomaly." Association with "cervicomedullary neuroschisis and mirror movement (synkinesia)". [chen p.10-11]

## Trauma & classifications
- "Fracture of the CVJ accounts for approximately 25% of cervical spine fracture cases." [chen p.11]
- Clivus fracture: "usually associated with high-energy blunt head trauma. Longitudinal, transverse, and oblique types have been described." "Mortality is high because of brain stem trauma or vertebrobasilar occlusion." Cranial nerves VI and VII deficits. [chen p.11]
- Occipital condyle fracture: "high-energy blunt trauma with axial compression, lateral bending, or rotational injury to the alar ligament." "Paralysis of the ninth, 10th, 11th, and 12th nerves is known as Collet-Sicard syndrome". Three types (Table 3, Anderson-Montesano per ref 28 and Tuli per ref 27, not named in text): I comminuted, axial compression, alar ligament and tectorial membrane preserved, stable; II condyle and extensive basioccipital fracture, direct blow, preserved, stable; III avulsion, forced rotation and lateral bending, disrupted, unstable. "An alternative classification divides these fractures into displaced and stable (ie, with and without ligamentous injury)". [chen p.11-12]
- Atlanto-occipital dislocation: "complete or near-complete disruption of the ligament between the occiput and upper cervical spine after hyperextension"; "type I odontoid fractures are usually found"; "incidence ... is higher in children"; "joints, which are more horizontal ... ligaments, which are weaker than in adults"; mortality "highly related to brain stem injury". Three types (ref Traynelis, not named in text): "anterior displacement of the occiput (type I), longitudinal distraction with separation of the occiput from C1 (type II), and posterior displacement of the occiput with respect to C1 (type III)." [chen p.12]
- Jefferson's fracture: "simultaneous disruption of the anterior and posterior arches of C1 with or without disruption of the supporting ligaments." Fall from a height most common, diving second. "If the transverse ligament remains intact, this fracture is stable. If the transverse ligament is disrupted, stability depends on the integrity of the alar ligament (Fig. 22)." Clinical: neck and suboccipital pain, dysphagia (retropharyngeal hematoma), posterior fossa stroke/TIA; "Neurologic deficits are extremely rare in cases of isolated C1 fracture." [chen p.12] (No Landells/Spence rule or measurement of lateral mass overhang in source.)
- Odontoid fracture: "approximately 15% all cervical fractures"; "hyperflexion injury is believed to play a major role"; three types (Fig. 23, ref Anderson-D'Alonzo): "Type I is defined as an avulsion fracture of the tip of the dens at the insertion site of the alar ligament. Type 2 is defined as a fracture at or above the junction of the dens and the central body. Type 3 is defined as a fracture in the superior body and superior articular facets of C2." [chen p.13-14] Type I MRI Fig. 24: "Posterior dislocation of the axis may be attributable to associated rupture of the transverse ligament." [chen p.14] Type III Fig. 25: "fracture at the low dens, resulting in rupture of the transverse ligament and causing C1-2 dislocation and compression to the cord"; "Apical and alar ligaments are intact but displaced along with the upper fragment." [chen p.15] Fig. 26 teardrop fracture of the posteroinferior odontoid, "better than the plain radiography". [chen p.15]
- Hangman's fracture (Effendi, ref 32): "a hyperextension fracture involving the pars interarticularis". "Type 1 is defined as an isolated minimally displaced fracture of the ring without displacement of the body or facets. Type 2 is defined as a fracture of the pars with anterior displacement of the axial body. Type 3 is defined as a fracture of the pars with anterior displacement of the axial body in the direction of flexion and dislocation of the bilateral facets. A type 2 lesion is more unstable and is usually considered to be a hyperextension-flexion injury. On imaging, the displacement can be more than 3 mm and associated with angulation of C2 or C3 disk space. The anterior and posterior longitudinal ligament and disk may be disrupted in type 2". [chen p.13] (Levine-Edwards modifications not mentioned.)

## Rheumatoid/inflammatory
- "RA involves the cervical spine in 60% to 70% of cases of RA." "The atlantoaxial joint is affected in 20% to 25% of patients who have RA". Mechanism: synovial inflammation, pannus, "Hyperemia causes weakening of the ligament and erodes the bones"; erosion gives the dens "a 'pencil-tip' appearance (Fig. 28)". "Inflammation leads to weakening of transverse and alar ligaments, and atlantoaxial subluxation can occur." [chen p.13-14]
- MRI in RA: "demonstrates the erosion of the dens, widening of the atlantodental space, degree of subluxation, reactive osteophytes, and configuration of the CVJ." [chen p.14] No ADI/PADI numeric values. Retrodental mass differential: CPPD (Fig. 29), psoriasis, amyloid arthropathy, "pseudotumors of the CVJ attributable to chronic mechanical instability". [chen p.14-15]
- Ankylosing spondylitis: "Spinal involvement occurs in approximately 50% of patients"; "shiny corner sign or Romanus lesion"; MRI: "small regions of low signal intensity on T1-weighted images and high signal intensity on T2-weighted images at the vertebral body corners"; "MR imaging is better in early detection of diskovertebral change and for grading the extent of enthesitis." [chen p.15]
- PVNS: "joint effusion; low signal intensity on T1-weighted and T2-weighted images; and gradient echo images secondary to hemosiderin deposition, hyperplastic synovium, bone erosions, and preservation of bone density." "Abnormal enhancement may be seen". [chen p.16]
- Differential DISH vs AS: "DISH can be differentiated from ankylosing spondylitis by the presence of marginal syndesmophytes and the involvement of sacroiliac joints" [sic as printed]. [chen p.20]

## Infection incl TB
- "Infection of the CVJ may result from inadequate treatment of a contiguous infection, such as otitis externa, paranasal sinusitis, or dental caries." Synovial lining "receives afferents from the paranasal sinuses and nasopharyngeal and retropharyngeal areas". "Previous radiotherapy to the head and neck is a predisposing factor" (Fig. 42). [chen p.26]
- "CVJ TB is a rare infection (only 0.3%-1% of spinal TB) that begins in the retropharyngeal space with secondary involvement of bone or, rarely, in the bone itself." "The imaging findings of CVJ infection include bony erosion and ligament destruction leading to instability at the CVJ and phlegmon or abscess formation in the paravertebral and epidural regions." [chen p.26]
- Table 5 (TB vs RA atlantoaxial dislocation): source - retropharyngeal TB vs chronic inflammation and vasculitis; mechanism of instability - "Destruction of ligaments" vs "Loss of tensile strength of transverse ligament"; process - "Infiltration" vs "Breakage"; additional - "Destruction of bone, additional compression attributable to cold abscess and granulation tissue" vs "Erosive changes in the adjacent bone, formation of granulation tissue in synovial joints". [chen p.26]
- Fig. 42 (post-radiation NPC pyogenic CVJ infection): rim enhancement "suggestive of central liquefaction"; CT "nonenhancing collection and gas inside. The spinal cord is compressed." [chen p.25]
- Pyogenic example in Box 1: Grisel's syndrome; granulomatous; epidural abscess. [chen p.5]
(Tuli/Fielding TB classifications and TB management: not in source except citation of Teegala 2008, ref 58.)

## Neoplasm
- "Neoplasms of the CVJ mainly arise from extradural structures"; "the most common primary tumors in the CVJ are meningiomas and chordomas." Box 2 lists extradural (chordoma, benign bony, malignant bony, direct pharyngeal invasion) and intradural extramedullary (meningioma, nerve sheath, congenital cysts). [chen p.20-21]
- Chordoma: "arises from the clivus with associated lytic bone change" at CT; "intratumoral calcifications are common"; T1 "intermediate to low signal intensity and is easily recognized within the high-signal fatty marrow in the clivus"; "Classic intracranial chordoma shows bright signal intensity on T2-weighted images"; "moderate to marked enhancement"; "A honeycomb pattern of enhancement with intratumoral areas of low signal intensity is typical"; "can spread to involve the jugular fossa and foramen magnum and can cause erosion of C1 and other cervical vertebrae." [chen p.20-21]
- Fibrous dysplasia: "typical 'ground-glass' appearance on CT"; usually anterior cranial fossa, may involve inferior clivus. Aneurysmal bone cyst: "expansile cystic mass, a thin rim of calcification, bony trabeculae within the cyst on CT, and characteristic fluid-filled cavities on MR imaging". [chen p.21-22]
- Chondrosarcoma: main differential of chordoma; "typically located in the paramedian petroclival synchondrosis, but they rarely occur in the midline"; "Curvilinear calcifications indicative of chondroid matrix"; T1 hypointense, T2 bright; "Differentiation from chordomas is sometimes difficult". [chen p.22]
- Plasmacytoma: "extramedullary plasmacytoma" vs "solitary plasmacytoma of bone"; "likelihood of progression to multiple myeloma from isolated extramedullary plasmacytoma is lower than that from solitary plasmacytoma of bone"; "usually exhibit lower signal intensity on T2-weighted images"; "Pathologic examination is always necessary". [chen p.22-23]
- NPC: "Patients who have NPC rarely present with symptoms of CVJ tumor"; "In cases of CVJ bony lesions, the nasopharyngeal mucosa should always be carefully checked, because a small mucosal tumor could be overlooked (Fig. 38)." [chen p.23]
- Meningioma: "Approximately 3% of all meningiomas occur at the foramen magnum." "the most common tumors (70% of all benign tumors) occurring at the foramen magnum"; "approximately two thirds occur in women ... between the ages of 35 and 60 years"; "Most ... are strictly intradural. Ten percent have extradural extensions"; "attached at the anterior rim of the foramen magnum"; "higher incidence of incomplete removal of extradural as compared with intradural meningiomas". [chen p.23]
- Neurinoma: "second most common nonosseous tumors of the CVJ"; "Multiple upper cervical neurinomas characteristically occur in cases of neurofibromatosis"; T2 markedly high with central hypointensity; intense enhancement. [chen p.24]
- Congenital cysts: arachnoid (CSF signal), epidermoid ("restricted diffusion"), neurenteric ("Approximately 10% occur in the CVJ"; "hyperintense to CSF on T2-weighted images, presumably because of their high protein content, and show no enhancement"). [chen p.25-26]

## Syndromic (Down, Morquio, achondroplasia etc.)
- Box 1 lists "Metabolic (Morquio's syndrome, Conradi's syndrome, fetal warfarin syndrome, and renal rickets)" and "Genetic transformations (Down's syndrome, osteogenesis imperfect[a], achondroplasia, Paget's disease, and neurofibromatosis)". [chen p.5] No imaging/measurement text for these.
- Odontoid aplasia associated with "Morquio's syndrome, spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia, and metatrophic dwarfism" (ref 20 Tishler: atlas dislocation in mongolism). [chen p.9, 27]
- Achondroplasia and "Hurler's syndrome" mentioned as causes of primary/secondary BI. [chen p.4, 6]; multiple upper cervical neurinomas in neurofibromatosis [chen p.24].

## Degenerative/other
- Degenerative arthritis: "may occur in 4% to 32% of patients who have osteoarthritis elsewhere"; osteophytes (anterior arch of C1, facets, dens), joint space narrowing at lateral atlantoaxial joints; "CT is better at showing subchondral cysts and ligamentous calcification". [chen p.16]
- OPLL: "commonly seen in oriental populations"; "1% to 3% of cases of symptomatic cervical spine stenosis and myelopathy in Japanese patients and in 1.53% of Chinese patients"; "after the age of 40 years"; cervical 70% to 75%, thoracic 15% to 20%, lumbar 10%; four types: "(1) continuous (extending over several vertebral bodies), (2) segmental (multiple separate retrovertebral lesions), (3) mixed ... and (4) circumscribed (confined to the retrodiskal space). The segmental type is most commonly found. Usually, the continuous type is thicker, may contain bone marrow, and is most frequently associated with severe cord compression." MRI: "hypointense structure on T1-weighted images and T2-weighted images". Differential: "calcified herniated disk, calcified meningioma, arteriovenous malformation, and subacute epidural hematoma." "Myelopathy occurs more often in patients with midline OPLL, whereas radiculopathy occurs more often in patients with eccentric OPLL." [chen p.16-18]
- DISH: "nonmarginal syndesmophytes at four successive vertebral levels and commonly do not exhibit disk and joint narrowing"; "more common in white men aged 50 to 75 years"; "prevalence may be as high as 28%"; "Plain radiography is usually the study of choice." [chen p.19-20]

## Instability & management/surgical decision points
- (Source has little surgical content.) "The degree of instability must be assessed by flexion and extension studies using plain radiography, polytomography, or MR imaging." [chen p.9]
- Jefferson stability hinges on transverse ligament; "If the transverse ligament remains intact, this fracture is stable." [chen p.12]
- Occipital condyle fracture: stable (types I, II, ligaments preserved) vs unstable (type III, disrupted alar ligament/tectorial membrane). [chen p.12]
- Hangman's type 2 "more unstable"; MRI to define ligamentous injury. [chen p.13]
- Isolated ossiculum terminale "stable when isolated and of little clinical significance." [chen p.9]
- Klippel-Feil/occipitalization: "high risk for early instability or degenerative disease". [chen p.10]
- Neoplasm: imaging should establish "nature and resectability" and "craniovertebral stability"; foramen magnum meningioma extradural component "higher incidence of incomplete removal"; preoperative angiography/embolization if highly vascular. [chen p.20, 23]
