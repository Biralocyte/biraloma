## Anatomy & ligaments
- "The craniovertebral junction (CVJ) is a collective term that refers to the occiput (posterior skull base), atlas, axis, and supporting ligaments. It encloses the soft tissue structures of the cervicomedullary junction (medulla, spinal cord, and lower cranial nerves)." [C-2392, Background]
- "The “ pro-atlas” is derived from the cranial portion of the embryonic fourth occipital sclerotome. Its principal derivatives are the occipital condyles, the dorsal cranial articular facets of the atlas, and the tip of the odontoid. The “primitive atlas” is derived from the caudal portion of the fourth occipital and the cranial portion of the first cervical sclerotomes. Its principal derivatives are the neural arches and lateral masses of the atlas and the remainder of the odontoid process axis. The “primitive axis” is derived from the caudal portion of the first cervical sclerotome and the cranial portion of the second. Its principal derivatives are the body and neural arch of the axis and the C2-3 cervical disk." [C-2392, Imaging findings — Anatomy: Embryology]
- "The occiput is composed of three main portions sorrounding the foramen magnum: basioccipital (lower part of the clivus), exoccipital and supraoccipital (Fig. 1) The atlas is formed by three primary ossification sites: the anterior arch and the two neural arches (Fig. 2) The axis presents four ossification centers at birth: one for the odontoid process, one for the body, and one for each neural arch. A secondary ossification center appears in the apex of the odontoid process (ossiculum terminale) (Fig. 3)" [C-0882, Imaging findings]
- "The craniocervical junction(CCJ) is composed of three bone structures: atlas(C1), axis(C2) and the occiput. These structures are approximated at two major joints, the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints. The atlantoaxial joints enable head rotation around the dens axis, whereas the atlantooccipital joints primarily allow flexion, extension, and lateral bending. The CCJ flexible and complex anatomy also makes it the weakest region of the entire vertebral column." [C-2555, Background]
- "Apical, alar, and transverse ligaments provide further stabilization by allowing spinal column rotation ; this prevents posterior displacement of the dens in relation to the atlas." [C-2392, Imaging findings — Traumatic disorders]
- Landmarks: "Opisthion: the posterior margin of the foramen magnum Basion:  the anterior tip of the foramen magnum Spinolaminar line : line along the posterior margin of spinal canal Parts of the C1 : The C1 has anterior and posterior arches, paired lateral masses and transverse processes.The dens or the odontoid process is located where the body of a typical vertebra would be Tuberculum sellae :  ridged anterior part of the sphenoid Internal occipital protruberance : inner tuberosity in the squama occipitalis which represents the point of  venous confluence Dorsum sellae : prominent posterior part of the sphenoid body Clivus : sloping midline surface of the skull base anterior to the foramen magnum and posterior to the dorsum sellae Nasion: point where the frontal and two nasal bones meet Posterior axial line : line drawn along and tangential to the posterior cortex of the body of the axis and extended cranially" [C-2397, Findings]

## Imaging technique
- "RX: Lateral view: 85-90% of cervical injuries are evident in lateral view.   -          Check aligment: 3 imaginary contour lines: anterior, posterior and spinolaminar. -          Atlantodental interval: <3mm adult, <5 mm child. Suspect transverse ligament disruption if these limits are exceeded. -          Check prevertebral space. At level C2 < 7mm . If widened suspect hematoma secondary to a fracture. -           Check for fanning of the spinous processes. -           Check for an abrupt change in angulation." [C-2392, Imaging findings — Anatomy: Technics]
- "Odontoid / Open mouth view: to evaluate an area that is difficult to visualize in the lateral view. Alignment of the lateral masses of C1 with respect  to the odontoid process. Anteroposterior view: The least useful view from clinical standpoint. A straight line should connect the spinous processes bisecting the cervical spine. If this is not seen consider a rotation injury. Flexion and extension: views for assesment to soft  tissue injury. CT: If plain radiograph findings are negative but clinical suspicion for fracture is high, films should be followed by CT. MRI is more sensitive for soft tissue injury than radiography. US should be considered the initial imaging modality of choice for investigating the spinal cord and CVJ in newborns." [C-2392, Imaging findings — Anatomy: Technics]
- "The normal reference values currently in place are those that were determined on plain radiographic imaging. The accuracy of radiographic craniometric measurements, however, is deficient due to the lack of precision in the identification of bony landmarks 2 . Fortunately, this shortcoming does not apply to CT" [C-2397, Background]
- "only a subset of these is unaffected by the position of the neck at the time of imaging and is therefore significant as specific positioning might not be possible in an acute setting. Eleven such parameters have been examined by us, encompassing both linear and angular craniometry." [C-2397, Background]
- "Craniocervical anatomical derangements are subtle and therefore difficult to diagnose on lateral radiographs. This can be efficiently overcome by utilizing CT based norms. The advantage of  multiplanar reformation (MPR) images that CT confers allows direct visualization of the joint spaces and lesser ambiguity due to magnification 3 ." [C-2397, Conclusion]
- "Despite CT is obviously superior to MR in diagnosis of craniocervical junctional bone anomalies, these anomalies are recognized by MR as well [7]." [C-2555, Findings]
- "CT evaluates bones very well, but MRI is absolute necessary in joint, ligament, vascular and soft tissue evaluation." [C-2555, Conclusion]
- "A standard trauma series is composed of 5 views: cross-table lateral, swimmer's, oblique, odontoid, and anteroposterior If plain radiograph findings are negative but clinical suspicion for fracture is high, films should be followed by CT. The American College of Orthopaedic Surgeons now recommends routine cervical spine screening via CT scan instead of plain radiography. Low-dose multidetector CT scanning has been found to be as sensitive and specific as standard dose multidetector CT scans. Soft tissue injury (ie, unstable ligamentous injury) has traditionally been evaluated by flexion-extension views. MR is less dangerous and more sensitive for soft tissue injury than radiography." [C-2392, Imaging findings — Decision Making]
- "Two clinical decision-making criteria, the Canadian C-Spine Rules (CCR) and the National Emergency X-Radiography Utilization Group (NEXUS) criteria allow clinicians to "clear" low-risk patients of c-spine injury, obviating the need for radiography. To be clinically cleared using the CCR, a patient must be alert (GCS 15), not intoxicated, and not have a distracting injury (eg, long bone fracture, large laceration). The NEXUS criteria state that a patient with suspected c-spine injury can be cleared providing the following: No posterior midline cervical spine tenderness is present. No evidence of intoxication is present. The patient has a normal level of alertness. No focal neurologic deficit is present. The patient does not have a painful distracting injury." [C-2392, Imaging findings — Decision Making]

All the congenital abnormalities of the occiput cause basilar invagination. (Fig. 8)" [C-0882, Imaging findings — Some basic concepts]
- "Basilar invagination. Abnormally high position of vertebral column prolapsing into skull base.To evaluate: Chamberlain, McGregor,Fishgold lines." [C-2392, Imaging findings — Congenital malformations] (primary/secondary forms quoted under GAP FILLERS, Goel)
- Platybasia: "Platybasia . Anthropometric term referring to flattering of skull base.               May be associated with : basilar invagination              - Cord symptoms.              - Craniovertebral = clivus-canal angle becomes acute (<150º).              - Welcher basal angle= sphenoid angle > 140º              - Bowstring deformity of CVJ." [C-2392, Imaging findings]; "Platybasia identified it as an X-ray finding. The simplest definition is to determine the skull base flattened. Manifested by enlarged Welcher basal angle [5]. Platybasia may be asymptomatic if there isn't accompanying basilar invagination and any other anomalies of skull base. The most common accompanying anomaly when platybasia is detected is basilar invagination [3]." [C-2555, Findings]
- Atlanto-occipital assimilation: "Atlanto-occipital assimilation. Failure of segmentation between the fourth occipital sclerotome and the first cervical sclerotome, also know as occipittalization of the atlas. This represents the most common anomaly involving the CVJ encountered in 0’14%-0’25% of the population. May be partial or complete, producing basilar invagination.Usually clivuscanal angle reduction. Often the association of assimilation atlanto-occipital and fusion of the axis with the third vertebra cervical. Associated congenital abnormalities, present in approximately 20% of patients, include incomplet clefting of the nasal cartilage, cleft palate, congenital external ear deformities, cervical ribs, hypospadias, and urinary tract anomalies. Results in C1-C2 instability in 50% of patients." [C-2392, Imaging findings]; "Atlantooccipital assimilation: partial or complete failure of segmentation between the skull and the first cervical vertebrae, resulting in basilar invagination. May associate fusion of C2 and C3. May lead to atlantoaxial subluxation (50%) or even sudden death." [C-0882, Imaging findings]; "The assimilation may be limited to the anterior arch, the posterior arch, the lateral masses, or exist in combination. Atlas assimilation can be unilateral or bilateral. Unilateral atlas assimilation caused torticollis in children [9]. New anatomical studies indicate that atlas assimilation is more prevalent than thought [11]. Atlas assimilation can predispose to atlanto-axial dislocation [12]." [C-2555, Findings]
- Basiocciput hypoplasia: "Basiocciput hypoplasia . Shortering of the clivus. Violation of Chamberlain’s line.  Clivus-canal angle typically decreased. <150º." [C-2392, Imaging findings]; "Basiocciput hypoplasia: may be mild or severe; results in shortening of the clivus and violaton of the Chamberlain line, being always associated with basilar invagination." [C-0882, Imaging findings]; "Platybasia is not observed in most basilar invagination patients, so the Welcher basal angle is normal. ## Trauma & classifications
- "Most cervical spine fractures occur predominantly at 2 levels. 1/3 at the level of C2 , and 1/2 at the level of C6 or C7. Upper cervical spine= C1/2 (19-25%) : atlas (4%), odontoid (6%)/ lower cervical spine= C3-C7 (75-81%)/ multpile continuos fractures (15-20%)." [C-2392, Imaging findings — Traumatic disorders]
- Occipital condyle fractures (Anderson–Montesano): see GAP FILLERS. Also: "Occipital condyle fracture Vertical compression + lateral bending. Avulsion of the condylar process or a comminuted compression. Associated with significant head trauma, accompanied by cranial nerve deficits. -X-ray: difficult to delineate, and axial CT may be required to identify them." [C-2392, Imaging findings]
- Atlanto-occipital dislocation: "Atlanto occipital dislocation (fig. 17) (distraction injury): disruption of the tectorial membrane and the paired alar ligaments resulting in unstable injury, caused by rapid acceleration with either hyperextension or hyperflexion. May be associated with occipital condile fracture. The basion-dens interval is > 12 mm. (without traction) on the lateral radiograph. On CT, incongruity of articulation between occipital condyles and lateral masse of the atlas is seen." [C-0882, Imaging findings]; "- Complete disruption of all ligamentous relationships between the occiput and the atlas. - Death usually occurs immediately from stretching of the brainstem, which causes respiratory arrest. - X-ray : disassociation between the base of the occiput and the arch of C1 is seen. - Cervical traction is absolutely contraindicated, since further stretching of the brainstem can occur." [C-2392, Imaging findings]
- Atlas fractures: "Atlas (C1) fractures I - II stable -          Isolated fractures:anterior/posterior arch of C1. -          Anterior arch fractures usually are avulsion fractures from the anterior portion of the ring. Low morbidity rate and little clinical significance. III- Fracture through the lateral mass of C1. X-ray: asymmetric displacement of the mass from the rest of the vertebra is seen in the odontoid view. Low morbidity rate and little clinical significance. IV- Burst fracture of the ring of C1/Jefferson fracture. Most significant type of atlas fracture from a clinical standpoint because it is associated with neurologic impairment. Managed with cervical traction." [C-2392, Imaging findings] ; Jefferson and lateral mass separation: see GAP FILLERS.
- Odontoid (Anderson & D'Alonzo): "Odontoid process fracture : 11-13% c-spine injuries. Associations: atlanto-axial dislocations, Jefferson fracture.  Anderson & D’Alonzo Classification I Rare.  Avulsion of the tip of the dens, at the insertion site of the alar ligament, where is attached to C1.  Stable since the fracture line is above the transverse ligament. II Most common (59%). Through the base of the dens. Always unstable and poor healing, high prevalence of nonunion (64%) due to the limited vascular supply and small area of cancellous bone. ## Rheumatoid/inflammatory
- "60-90% pacients with rheumatoid arthritis have cervical spine involvement. -          A synovial capsule separates the dens axis from the transverse ligament. Rheumatoid synovitis in this joint leads to laxity of the transverse and alar ligaments and erosion of the subjacent bone. ... ATLANTOAXIAL ARTICULATION: subluxation, dens axis moving posteriorly and superiorly . -          Asymptomatic despite as a remarkable degree of suluxation. -           2/3 pacients: Dens axis erosion + adjacent soft tissue mass (composed of inflamed synovium and pannus as well as fibrous granulation tissue and contributes significantly to narrowing of the spinal canal.) -           RARE: Spontaneous fracture of the dens axis from the combination of erosion and osteoporosis + steroid therapy. -          Treatment: instability---fusion risk of medultary compression and sudden death," [C-2392, Imaging findings — Rheumatoid arthritis]
- "Reumathoid arthritis leads to development of laxity of the ligaments and joint capsules. Atlantoaxial subluxation can occur (fig. 32) when the transverse and alra ligaments become too lax. RA synovitis also leads to bone destrcution involving lateral masses of C1 (when assimetric, may result in atlantoaxial subluxation), the occipital condyles, and the articular facets of C2. As the destruction progresses, the odontoid process projectes into the foramen magnum (vertical subluxation). Radiographically, the odontoid is not visible on the lateral plain radiograph." [C-0882, Imaging findings — Inflammatory conditions]
- "Grisel's syndrome is defined as spontaneous subluxation of the atlantoaxial joints following parapharyngeal infection, due to ligamentous stretching, muscle spasm and hyperemia with decalcification of ligaments. Most frequently affects chidren under 12 years." [C-0882, Imaging findings]
- Ankylosing spondylitis: "-          Young adulthood. -          Lose mobility of the spine owing to production of “flowing syndesmophytes”, which effectively fuse the vertebral elements. -          TAUMA (minor): FRACTURE:displacement of fused spinal segments spinal canal compromise and severe neurologic consequences. -           PSEUDOARTHROSIS FORMATION or BONE DESTRUCTION at non fused segment." [C-2392, Imaging findings]
- Gout: "Documented urate deposition in the spine is exceedingly rare. Abnormalities in the cervical segment include erosions of the odontoid process or endplates of the vertebral bodies, disc space narrowing, and vertebral subluxation." [C-2392, Imaging findings]

## Instability & management
- "Stability at the craniocervical junction  ,  RX potential Instability signs Predental space > 5 mm in children <8 y./ >3 mm above. Separation of the atlas lateral masses  more than 6 mm. Cruciate ligament is felt to be disrupted. RX: open-mouth view , coronal CT.  Vertical translation between  clivus and  odontoid process > 2 mm = atlanto-occipital instability.  Gap between the occipital condyles and the atlas lateral facets is visible on a  lateral cervical radiograph.  Abnormal relationship between spinal canal and foramen magnum.  Abnormal craniocervical motion dynamics.  Disrupted transverse, cruciate, alar ligaments and tectorial membrane.  Harris rule of 12’s:  distance from  basion to posterior axial line ( drawn posterior body of C2), and distance from  basion to the tip, >12mm indicates instability." [C-2392, Imaging findings]
